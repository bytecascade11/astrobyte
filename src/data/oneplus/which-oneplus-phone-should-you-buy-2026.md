## Overview 

OnePlus doesn't sell one flagship anymore. It sells five, and they all overlap in price at some point during the year. That's the actual problem people run into when they search "which OnePlus phone should I buy" — not a lack of good options, but too many good options fighting over the same budget.

## Quick answer

- **Best overall:** OnePlus 15
- **Best value among the current OnePlus lineup:** OnePlus 15R
- **Best compact:** OnePlus 13s
- **Best older flagship, if discounted:** OnePlus 13
- **Best budget pick:** OnePlus 13R

The reasoning behind each of those calls is below.

Right now the active lineup is the OnePlus 15, the OnePlus 15R, the OnePlus 13, the OnePlus 13R, and the compact OnePlus 13s. A sixth phone, the OnePlus 15T, exists but launched in China only in March 2026 with no confirmed global rollout — its China-only status also makes global network compatibility, warranty coverage, and long-term support harder to assess, so it's left out of the buying advice below.

## OnePlus 15 — buy this if you want the best OnePlus makes right now

The 15 is the phone to get if you're not trying to save money, you're trying to get the newest chip and the longest software runway. It's OnePlus's first phone built around the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 — not necessarily the first phone from any manufacturer with that chip, since several other Android makers launched Elite Gen 5 devices around the same window, but it is the newest silicon OnePlus has shipped. OnePlus also dropped the Hasselblad camera branding this generation in favor of its own in-house DetailMax imaging engine; the rear camera is still a triple 50MP setup (main, ultrawide, periscope telephoto), but tuning is now handled entirely by OnePlus rather than Hasselblad.

Where it separates from the 15R below it: a faster chipset, a display that scales its refresh rate down further to save battery, IP68/IP69/IP69K water and dust resistance, and flagship-tier build materials. If you're coming from a phone that's three or four years old and want something that won't feel dated by 2029, this is the one.

## OnePlus 15R — the value pick, with one real trade-off

The 15R undercuts the 15 by $200 while matching a lot of what makes the 15 good: the same 165Hz-class display, a slightly bigger 7,400mAh battery, and the same 12GB/256GB base configuration. Its Snapdragon 8 Gen 5 chip benchmarks meaningfully behind the 15's Elite Gen 5 in raw scores, but in day-to-day use — social apps, video, most games — that gap is hard to feel. It also carries the same broad IP66/68/69/69K rating set as the 15.

Its biggest compromise is the camera. The 15R drops the 15's ultrawide-plus-telephoto setup down to a single 50MP main and an 8MP ultrawide, with no telephoto lens at all — a meaningful cut if you shoot a lot of zoom or portrait shots. If photography matters more to you than raw performance or battery size, this isn't the pick — go up to the 15, or sideways to the 13R, which keeps a 50MP telephoto the 15R skips entirely.

If you're weighing battery-first phones from other brands too, it's worth putting the 15R's 7,400mAh cell next to what [Huawei's battery-focused lineup](https://www.revibyte.blog/huawei/best-huawei-phones-battery-life-2026/) is doing at a similar price — Huawei tends to prioritize raw capacity over refresh rate, which is a different set of trade-offs entirely.

## OnePlus 13s — the one for people who miss small phones

Most 2026 flagships have settled into the 6.7–6.8-inch range, which makes the 13s an outlier worth mentioning even though it's harder to source outside India. It packs a full Snapdragon 8 Elite chip and a 5,850mAh battery into a 6.32-inch, 185g body — for comparison, that's a larger battery than most phones its size manage while weighing less than an iPhone 16 Pro.

The camera is the clearest compromise: a 50MP main paired with a 50MP 2x telephoto, and no ultrawide lens at all. IP65 is also a step down from the fuller IP68/IP69/IP69K ratings on the 15-series phones. If your priority is one-handed use over camera versatility, it's a rare option in a market that's mostly stopped making compact flagships — just budget for import costs on top of the India sticker price.

## OnePlus 13 and 13R — still worth buying at the right price

The OnePlus 13 remains a capable phone in 2026 largely because its Snapdragon 8 Elite chip hasn't aged in any way that matters yet, and its 6,000mAh battery plus QHD+ LTPO display still hold up against phones released a year later. It makes the most sense now as a discounted or secondhand buy rather than at original pricing, since the 15 series has since matched or exceeded most of what made it worth $899.99 at launch.

The 13R sits at the bottom of the current flagship-adjacent lineup and is the phone people point to when they say OnePlus still makes "flagship killers." Its Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 is a generation behind, but it keeps a 50MP telephoto lens and an LTPO display — a telephoto the newer, pricier 15R doesn't have, which is a genuinely odd gap in an otherwise more expensive phone. Coverage reports on the 13R put its software support at four Android version upgrades — through Android 19 — with security patches continuing into early 2031, so longevity isn't the concern here; camera versatility for the price is the actual selling point.

## A note on buying OnePlus in Nigeria

OnePlus has limited official retail availability in Nigeria, so most Nigerian buyers purchase through importers, diaspora relatives, or local resellers bringing in UK or Indian-market units. That affects two things buyers often overlook: warranty coverage can be limited or unavailable locally for imported units, so check both the seller's and manufacturer's warranty terms before buying — don't assume a UK or India warranty travels with the phone. It's also worth double-checking network band compatibility against Nigerian carriers before paying, since a UK unit and an Indian unit aren't always identical in radio hardware.

## The OnePlus phone I'd buy

If money isn't the concern, get the OnePlus 15 — it's the newest chip OnePlus has shipped and offers the longest software and performance runway. For most buyers, though, the 15R is the more compelling deal: it preserves most of the flagship experience for $200 less, and the camera cut only matters if you actually shoot a lot of zoom shots. The 13s makes sense if a smaller phone in your hand is worth the import hassle, while the 13 and 13R become genuinely interesting the moment either one shows up discounted — the 13R in particular, if you can find it at a genuinely discounted price.

## FAQ

**Is the OnePlus 15 worth $200 more than the OnePlus 15R?**
Only if performance headroom or camera quality matters to you specifically. For screen quality, battery life, and everyday speed, the gap is small enough that most buyers won't notice it.

**Which OnePlus phone has the best battery life in 2026?**
The OnePlus 15R, at 7,400mAh, edges out the OnePlus 15's 7,300mAh cell on paper. The larger battery doesn't automatically guarantee dramatically better endurance, though, because real-world battery life also depends on the display, chipset, and software.

**Is the OnePlus 13R still worth buying in 2026?**
Yes, mainly for its price and camera versatility — it keeps a telephoto lens some newer, pricier OnePlus phones drop, though its Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 chip is now two generations behind.

**Can I buy the OnePlus 13s outside India?**
Not officially. It launched as an India exclusive, so anyone outside that market is relying on importers, which affects both price and warranty support.

**Does OnePlus sell phones in Nigeria officially?**
Official retail availability is limited. Most units sold locally come through import channels, so check network band compatibility and confirm warranty terms with your seller before buying.
