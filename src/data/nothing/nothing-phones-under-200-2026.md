## Overview 

Search for a Nothing phone under $200 and you'll quickly find listings that disagree on what these phones actually cost. One site says a Nothing phone costs $199. Another swears the same model is $290. A Nigerian classifieds page prices it at one figure, while a comparison site quotes something several times higher for what looks like the exact same device. None of these people are lying, exactly. They're just describing different things — launch price, current street price, a Nigeria-only markup, or a secondhand unit that's been through two owners. This guide sorts through that noise and tells you what's genuinely available under $200 right now, and what isn't.

**A quick definition before we start:** "under $200" here means the phone's base retail price at official launch, before Nigerian import costs, taxes, delivery, or accessory bundles are added. Naira figures below are rough conversions and reseller listings as of September 2026 — check the exchange rate on the day you're buying, since it moves.

## The short answer: it's CMF, not "Nothing"

Nothing no longer really sells phones around the $200 mark. Its mainline devices — the Phone (2a), the Phone (3a) family, and the newer Phone (4a) — all launch well above that mark. The Phone (4a) Pro, for instance, launched in the US in March 2026 at $499, according to reporting from 9to5Google and BGR at the time; BGR's coverage in late August 2026 notes the price has since dropped to $449. Either way, it's nowhere near $200. If you want the transparent-design aesthetic Nothing is known for, at under $200, you're shopping the CMF sub-brand instead, or hunting for an older Nothing Phone (1) on the secondhand market.

CMF Phone 1 is the closest thing to a true under-$200 Nothing-family device. Its original US launch price was $199 for 8GB/128GB, making it one of the few Nothing-family phones that fits this budget. It's not flashy — a MediaTek Dimensity 7300, a 6.67-inch 120Hz AMOLED screen, a 5,000mAh battery, and a 50MP main camera — but for the price, it's one of the few budget Android phones that doesn't feel like it's cutting corners on the display.

![CMF Phone 1 rear panel with the interchangeable back cover partially removed](/assets/nothing/cmf-phone-1-back-panel.jpg)

Worth flagging directly, since it affects whether this phone is worth planning around: the CMF Phone 2 Pro, launched in 2025, remains the latest CMF phone as of September 2026, and as of writing, neither current CMF model is listed for sale on Nothing's own website. In June 2026, Nothing co-founder Akis Evangelidis confirmed on X that the company had decided not to launch a new CMF phone in 2026, citing the rising cost of phone memory as the reason a successor to the CMF Phone 2 Pro couldn't hit its target price — a statement first reported by BGR in August 2026. That doesn't mean existing CMF Phone 1 stock is bad or unsafe to buy; it means remaining inventory is what's left, not an ongoing restock, and BGR reported that stock had "gradually disappeared" in some markets, with certain regions already sold out.

## What Nigerian buyers are actually seeing

This is where the price confusion gets worse, not better, and it's worth explaining why rather than just handing you a naira figure. Nigerian marketplace listings for these phones vary hugely because they're describing different things: an official import versus a "UK used" unit, a bundle that throws in earbuds, or a listing that's simply outdated because the model isn't being restocked. If you see a price that looks unusually low for a CMF Phone 1, treat that as a signal to ask more questions, not a reason to get excited — it likely means a used, incomplete, or bundled-with-conditions listing, not a genuine new-in-box deal. The safest approach is to convert the $199 US launch price at the day's exchange rate as your reference point, then compare specific listings against that, rather than trusting any single "Nigeria price" figure you find online.

If your budget genuinely caps at $200 and you want something newer than the original Nothing Phone (1), the CMF Phone 1 is the realistic pick. If you're open to buying used, a well-kept Nothing Phone (1) gets you the brand's signature look for less, at the cost of an older chipset and shorter remaining software support.

## Is a $200 Nothing phone actually worth it?

For the design and the screen, yes — the 120Hz AMOLED display is one of the CMF Phone 1's strongest features at this price. CMF's relatively short software-support commitment is worth considering if you plan to keep the phone for several years, especially alongside the update policies of bigger players — see how [Samsung's update policy](https://www.revibyte.blog/samsung/how-long-do-samsung-phones-get-updates/) compares. If you keep phones for three-plus years, that's worth weighing against the upfront savings.

## Buying tips if you're shopping under $200 in Nigeria

- Confirm whether the unit is global/UK spec or a regional variant — some CMF units sold locally are missing bands used by certain Nigerian carriers.
- Ask for the IMEI before paying and check it against the phone's box, not just a screenshot.
- Bundled deals (phone plus earbuds) can look cheaper per item but often push the total above $200 once converted from naira.
- Warranty support for CMF devices in Nigeria is inconsistent; get a written return window from the seller if buying secondhand.

## FAQ

**Is there a brand-new Nothing-branded phone under $200 right now?**
Not from the mainline Nothing series — the Phone (4a) Pro alone starts at $499. The CMF Phone 1, a Nothing sub-brand, is the one that launched at $199.

**Is the CMF Phone 1 still available in 2026?**
In some markets, yes, but availability is becoming more limited as Nothing has confirmed that no new CMF phone will launch in 2026. Check current listings before assuming a unit is in stock.

**Should I buy a used Nothing Phone (1) instead of a new CMF Phone 1?**
Only if the price gap is significant and you're comfortable with an older Snapdragon 778G+ chipset and fewer remaining software updates. Otherwise, the CMF Phone 1 is the newer, better-supported option — for as long as remaining stock lasts.

**Does the CMF Phone 2 Pro count as "under $200"?**
No — it launched at $279 for the base 8GB/128GB model. It's a genuine step up in camera and display quality over the CMF Phone 1, but it sits outside this budget.

**What should I check before buying one in Nigeria?**
IMEI verification, band compatibility with your carrier, and a clear return policy — grey-market pricing swings are common enough that "cheap" doesn't always mean "good deal."
