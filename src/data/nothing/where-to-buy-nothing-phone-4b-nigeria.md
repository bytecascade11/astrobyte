## Overview 

The Nothing Phone (4b) arrived in July 2026 as the entry point to Nothing's Phone (4) range, priced below the (4a), and a fair number of Nigerians have asked the same thing since: where do I actually get one?

The honest answer is that there is no confirmed official route yet. Nothing has not announced Nigerian pricing or an authorised distributor for this model, so anything you find here today is coming through a marketplace seller, a phone market stall or a private import. That is not automatically a problem. It does mean you are the one carrying the risk, so it is worth knowing where the risk sits.

Nigerian listings for this phone are all over the place, so start with prices that come from the manufacturer's own markets. Nigerian price directories we checked disagreed with each other by a factor of more than ten, which tells you most of them are placeholders or conversions rather than real shop prices.

Two notes on those figures. Indian pricing is muddier than the table suggests: launch coverage gives ₹34,999, first-sale offers on Flipkart were reported to bring it to about ₹29,999, and some Indian retailers list far higher sticker prices with large discounts, so treat the Indian number as a ballpark. And whatever you pay in Nigeria will sit above the European and Indian numbers once a seller adds shipping, import duty, their margin and a cushion for exchange-rate swings. We could not find a trustworthy Nigerian shop price for the (4b), so we are not guessing one.

### Marketplaces

Jumia is the easiest place to start because it offers delivery and, on many items, payment on delivery. When we checked on 6 November 2026, Jumia's Nothing page showed six listings: the Phone (4a), (4a) Pro, Phone (3) and Phone (2). There was no (4b). Search again before you assume. Third-party sellers on any marketplace set their own prices and their own return terms, so read the seller's page, not just the product page.

### Classifieds and markets

Jiji and Instagram sellers sometimes get stock earlier than formal retail does. They also produce the most horror stories. The Jiji pages we could find showed older Nothing and CMF models, and we could not verify a specific (4b) listing. If you do spot one, treat the price as the seller's ask, and note whether the seller is a verified business, which storage size and version it is, and when the listing went up. Meet in a public place, open the box yourself, and do not transfer money for a phone you have not held. In the Ikeja market, ask where the unit came from. A seller who can tell you "India" or "UK" and shows a matching box label is more believable than one who just says "original."

### Importing

If a relative is travelling to the UK, Europe, India or Hong Kong, buying abroad often gives the cleanest result, because you know exactly which version you get. Just remember that a foreign receipt rarely helps you with a repair in Lagos or Abuja.

Band lists are from GSMArena, a secondary source; Nothing's own spec page is the better reference if you can open it. Read them as a comparison, not a guarantee. The international version supports noticeably more LTE bands, including 7 and 20, which the India list omits. Both versions list 5G band n78. Which bands your network actually uses for 4G and 5G varies by carrier and by city, so ask your operator, and do not assume either version will perform equally well on every Nigerian network.

Ask the seller which version they are selling. If they cannot tell you, assume you will not get the larger battery, and be more careful about band support. ## The NCC Device Checker rollout: what buyers should know

From 7 October 2026, the NCC has been enforcing type approval and device verification more strictly. Type approval applies to a phone model, and verification applies to each individual handset through its IMEI. According to the NCC's [official Device Checker FAQ](https://ncc.gov.ng/frequently-asked-questions/ncc-device-checker-and-type-approval), phones already connected to a Nigerian network before that date were verified automatically, while a new device brought in by an importer must now be verified. The same FAQ says that if you buy from a seller in Nigeria, getting type approval is not your job; that duty sits with the manufacturers, importers and suppliers. Your job is to check before you pay.

We could not confirm whether the Phone (4b) has Nigerian type approval, so run the check yourself. Dial *315# on the phone and choose the option to check the device, or use the online checker at ncc.gov.ng/devicechecker, which may ask for the 15-digit IMEI if you are checking a different phone. You want a result that says the device is type-approved and verified for use on Nigerian mobile networks.

Three cautions from the NCC's own guidance:

- **A failed check is not proof of a fake.** The NCC says a device that cannot be verified is not automatically confirmed to be counterfeit, but it also says not to pay until the seller can explain and fix the result. A successful result does not guarantee the phone's condition, repair history or every component either.
- **Personal imports follow a different process.** If you bought the phone abroad for your own use, the NCC says a different verification route may apply and tells you to call 315 for a step-by-step guide. For phones never used in Nigeria, the FAQ also describes a limited grace period of up to 30 cumulative days in a year for visitors.
- **Watch for scams.** Use only *315# and the official NCC page. If the process sends you a one-time code, enter it only in that process and never read it out to a seller or caller.

If something goes wrong with a check or with network access, the NCC lists 315 for Device Checker help and 622 for the Commission.

## Check these before you pay

- Check the exact model number and regional variant on the box and in Settings.
- Run the NCC check described above (*315# on the phone, with your SIM inserted) before any money moves.
- Dial *#06# and compare the IMEI with the one on the box. A match is a basic check only; it does not prove the phone is genuine.
- Ask for the seller's return policy in writing, and find out whether the warranty is manufacturer-backed or the seller's own.
- Look for a sealed box, a charger or cable in the right format, and no signs of a previous activation.
- Insert your SIM and test calls, mobile data, Wi-Fi, charging, both cameras, the speakers and the fingerprint sensor in front of the seller.
- Ask for a written receipt that names the exact model and storage size.
- Compare the 5G and 4G bands for that version against your network's before you commit, particularly with a foreign variant.

## FAQ

### Is the Nothing Phone (4b) officially sold in Nigeria?

Not as far as we could confirm. Nothing has not announced Nigerian pricing or an authorised retailer for this model. Check nothing.tech before buying, because that could change.

### How much does the Nothing Phone (4b) cost in Nigeria?

There is no official Nigerian price. Directory sites list figures that disagree wildly, so do not trust any single number. Compare the European and Indian launch prices above, then add shipping, duty and the seller's margin.

### Is it safe to buy a Nothing phone on Jiji or Instagram?

It can be, if you inspect the phone in person, verify the IMEI and avoid paying before you hold it. Never rely on photos alone.

### Will the Nothing Phone (4b) work on MTN, Airtel and Glo?

Most modern phones connect on Nigerian networks, but 5G band support varies by version. Check the band list for the specific model you are buying against your carrier's coverage. GSMArena lists different network bands for the India and international versions, and the India list has fewer LTE bands. Also run the NCC *315# check, since type approval and verification now apply to newly imported devices.

### What if the NCC Device Checker does not verify a Phone (4b)?

Do not pay until the seller explains and resolves it. The NCC says an unsuccessful check does not automatically mean the phone is counterfeit, but unverified devices can be refused network access. If you imported the phone yourself, call 315 for the personal-import process.

### Does the Nothing Phone (4b) get software updates?

Nothing has promised three years of Android OS updates and six years of security patches, according to launch reporting.

### Which version should I choose, India or international?

If you can choose, the India version's larger reported battery is the main advantage. If you cannot confirm the version, treat the 5,200mAh figure as your baseline.
