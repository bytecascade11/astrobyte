## Overview 

If you've walked through Computer Village or scrolled Jiji.ng lately, you'll notice TECNO isn't hiding in Samsung's shadow anymore, at least not when it comes to cameras. The Camon line has become one of TECNO's strongest options for Nigerians who want good cameras without paying flagship prices.  
  
The catch is that TECNO now ships so many Camon and Phantom variants — Pro, Pro 5G, Ultra, Premier — that half the "latest" listings you'll see online are actually last year's stock repackaged with a new sticker, and some retailer copy blurs specs between models that aren't actually identical. So I went through GSMArena's device listings and TECNO's own spec sheets for each model below, cross-checked them against current retailer pricing, and corrected a few things that don't match the marketing copy.

## 1. TECNO Camon 50 Ultra — best overall camera package  
  
**Camera strengths:** A 50MP autofocus selfie camera (genuinely useful, since most mid-rangers still ship fixed-focus front cameras that struggle with group shots), a Sony LYT700C main sensor with OIS, and a 50MP 3x optical telephoto that also carries OIS. GSMArena's review notes all three rear cameras support 4K video, which is not the case on the Pro models.  
  
**Camera weaknesses:** The rear camera hardware is shared with the Camon 50 Pro 5G — the Ultra's advantage is almost entirely in the selfie camera and video capture ceiling, not in the rear photos themselves.  
  
**Why it's worth the price:** If you shoot a lot of selfies or vlog-style front-camera video, the autofocus front sensor and 4K support across all cameras are real, measurable upgrades.  
  
**Who should skip it:** Anyone who mostly shoots with the rear camera and doesn't need 4K footage — you're paying a premium for a badge and a better front camera you may rarely use.  
  
It runs on the Dimensity 7400 Ultimate, carries a 6,500mAh battery with 45W charging. TECNO also rates it for IP68/IP69K protection and MIL-STD-810 durability.  
  
## 2. TECNO Camon 50 Pro 5G — the value pick  
  
**Camera strengths:** Per GSMArena's and TECNO's own spec listings, the Pro 5G carries essentially the same rear-camera configuration as the Ultra — a 50MP LYT700C main sensor with OIS, a 50MP 3x optical telephoto, and an 8MP ultrawide. The published specs match; whether every underlying component is identical across regional variants isn't something I can confirm. On paper, there should be little practical difference in rear-camera hardware.  
  
**Camera weaknesses:** The front camera drops to a fixed-focus 32MP sensor (versus the Ultra's 50MP autofocus unit), and video capture tops out lower than the Ultra's across the secondary lenses.  
  
**Why it's worth the price:** You're getting most of the rear-camera hardware for considerably less money than the Ultra. For photo-first buyers who don't care about the front camera or 4K ceiling, this is where the value actually sits.  
  
**Who should skip it:** Heavy selfie-takers or anyone planning to shoot a lot of 4K front-facing video — that's specifically what the Ultra adds.  
  
This is the phone I'd point most Nigerian buyers toward first, before considering the Ultra.

## 3. TECNO Camon 50 Pro 4G — flagship glass, lower entry cost  
  
**Camera strengths:** According to GSMArena's spec sheet, the 4G Pro carries the same 50MP LYT700C main sensor with OIS and the same 50MP 3x optical telephoto (also with OIS on this variant) as its 5G sibling, plus the 8MP ultrawide.  
  
**Camera weaknesses:** Fixed-focus 32MP selfie camera, and no 5G connectivity if that matters for your carrier or region.  
  
**Why it's worth the price:** If 5G isn't a priority yet — reasonable, given how patchy coverage still is outside Lagos, Abuja, and Port Harcourt — you get essentially the same rear camera experience at a meaningfully lower price.  
  
**Who should skip it:** Anyone in an active 5G zone who wants to future-proof their next 2-3 years of network access.

I need to correct something here: earlier marketing language (including some of my own first draft) associated this phone with TECNO's older "gimbal camera" branding. That feature belonged to the 2021 Camon 18 Premier, not this model. GSMArena's own review of the Camon 40 Premier actually flags mediocre video stabilization as one of its weak points, so don't buy this one expecting gimbal-smooth handheld video.  
  
**Camera strengths:** What it does have is genuinely unusual — a 50MP main (OIS), 50MP 3x optical telephoto, and 50MP ultrawide, all at the same resolution. Most competitors, including TECNO's own Camon 50 series, drop the ultrawide down to 8MP. The front camera is also a 50MP autofocus sensor, matching the pricier Camon 50 Ultra.  
  
**Camera weaknesses:** GSMArena's review specifically calls out weaker video stabilization and softer 2x crop zoom quality compared to its stills performance.  
  
**Why it's worth the price:** If wide-angle detail matters to you — architecture, landscapes, group shots — the 50MP ultrawide here outresolves every other phone on this list.  
  
**Who should skip it:** Anyone prioritizing smooth video capture, and anyone buying used or "new-old-stock" units without inspecting them first, since availability and stock condition are inconsistent right now.  
  
## 5. TECNO Camon 50 (4G) — best under ₦400k  
  
**Camera strengths:** A 50MP main sensor with OIS carries most of the photographic weight here, backed by an 8MP autofocus ultrawide.  
  
**Camera weaknesses:** This is a dual rear camera, not a triple — there's no telephoto lens at all, so zoomed shots rely entirely on digital cropping. The selfie camera is a fixed-focus 32MP sensor, not the 50MP unit sometimes implied by "shares the Camon 50 name" marketing.  
  
**Why it's worth the price:** For anyone who mainly shoots wide and doesn't miss optical zoom, this is a real 50MP OIS main camera without stretching into Pro-tier pricing. The 6.78-inch 144Hz AMOLED display and 6,150mAh battery are also generous for the price bracket.  
  
**Who should skip it:** Anyone who wants optical zoom for portraits or distant subjects — step up to the Camon 50 Pro 4G for that.

## Which one should you actually buy?  
  
- **Want the single best camera TECNO sells and don't mind paying for it:** Camon 50 Ultra.  
- **Want most of that camera hardware for noticeably less:** Camon 50 Pro 5G — this is the one most people should buy.  
- **On a tighter budget but still want the Pro camera hardware:** Camon 50 Pro 4G.  
- **Care more about ultrawide detail than smooth video:** Camon 40 Premier 5G, if you can find a genuinely new unit at a fair price.  
- **Just want a solid single-lens camera under ₦400,000 and don't need zoom:** Camon 50 (4G).

## Where to buy safely  
  
Jumia's official TECNO store and Slot/Pointek physical outlets are the most reliable for warranty-backed units. Jiji.ng often has the lowest listed prices, but that's also where mislabeled units (an older Camon 40 Pro sold as "Camon 50 Pro," for instance) tend to surface — always verify the IMEI against the box and ask for the original TECNO warranty card before paying.  
  
## FAQ  
  
**Which TECNO phone has the best camera in Nigeria right now?**  
The Camon 50 Ultra has the strongest overall camera package, mainly because of its 50MP autofocus selfie camera and 4K video support across all three rear lenses — though it doesn't win every individual category (the Camon 40 Premier 5G's 50MP ultrawide outresolves the Ultra's 8MP unit). The Camon 50 Pro 5G carries essentially the same rear-camera configuration for less money, so it's arguably the better buy unless the front camera specifically matters to you.  
  
**Is the Camon 50 series a big upgrade over the Camon 40 series for photography?**  
It depends which Camon 40 model you're comparing against. Against the base Camon 40 or Camon 40 Pro, yes — the Camon 50 Pro line adds a genuine 3x optical telephoto and OIS on the main sensor. Against the Camon 40 Premier 5G specifically, it's more of a lateral move: the Premier actually matches or beats the Camon 50 Pro on ultrawide resolution (50MP vs 8MP).  
  
**Do the IP68/IP69K ratings actually apply to all these phones, or just the flagship?**  
Based on GSMArena's individual spec listings, the Camon 50 (4G), Camon 50 Pro 4G, Camon 50 Pro 5G, and Camon 50 Ultra are each independently listed with IP68/IP69K dust and water resistance, and the Camon 40 Premier 5G carries IP68/IP69. It's not a blanket claim — each model happens to carry it, but always check the specific SKU you're buying, since regional variants can differ.  
  
**Do TECNO Camon phones support 5G in Nigeria?**  
The Pro 5G, Ultra, and Camon 40 Premier 5G variants support 5G connectivity. Actual speeds and coverage depend on which network you're on and how built-out 5G is in your area — coverage is still concentrated in major city centres, so outside those, a 4G variant won't feel like a meaningful downgrade in daily use. Confirm your carrier's specific 5G rollout in your area rather than assuming band compatibility guarantees usable speeds.  
  
**Are TECNO phones worth it compared to Infinix or Xiaomi at the same price?**  
For camera-first buyers, TECNO's Camon line has generally pulled ahead of Infinix's equivalent tier (unsurprising, since they share a parent company and TECNO tends to get the better sensors first) and holds its own against Xiaomi's offerings in the same bracket. Xiaomi tends to win on raw performance benchmarks; TECNO tends to win on camera consistency and battery life. This is a general pattern, not a guarantee for every specific model pairing — check individual comparisons before deciding.  
  
**How long will a TECNO Camon phone get software updates?**  
GSMArena's spec listings for the Camon 50 (4G), Camon 50 Pro 4G, and Camon 40 Premier 5G each note "up to 3 major Android upgrades," and TECNO's own Camon 50 Ultra review coverage mentions the same three-update promise. That's a real improvement over earlier Camon generations, though it's still short of what Samsung or Google promise at similar price points, and "up to" language means it isn't a guaranteed floor.  
  
**Is it safe to buy a TECNO camera phone used or on Jiji.ng?**  
It can be, but confirm the IMEI matches the box, check for an active warranty transfer where possible, and test the camera modules (especially the telephoto and any zoom function) in person before paying, since these are the components most likely to show wear or mismatched specs on a used or mislabeled unit.
