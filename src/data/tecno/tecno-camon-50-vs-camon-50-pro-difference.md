## Overview 

If you are standing in front of two TECNO phones that look almost identical and the price gap is real money, the honest question is simple: what do you get for the extra cost?

With the Camon 50 and Camon 50 Pro, the answer is shorter than TECNO's marketing suggests. Going by TECNO's published specs, the two phones share the same chipset, charging speed, screen size, refresh rate, main camera and, on the 4G models' official listings, battery size. The Pro changes two things you can actually see and feel: a curved display and a third rear camera.

This comparison covers the 4G Camon 50 Pro, the model TECNO Nigeria lists as the Pro. It is not the 5G Camon 50 Ultra, which is a different phone with a different chipset (covered further down).

Everything below is independently researched from spec sheets, not hands-on testing, so read the camera and battery sections as what the hardware allows rather than what we measured.

## The short version

Both phones run MediaTek's Helio G200 Ultimate with 8GB RAM and 256GB storage, a 6.78-inch AMOLED panel at 144Hz, and a 6,150mAh battery with 45W wired charging. Both carry IP68, IP69 and IP69K ratings and ship with Android 16 on HiOS 16. GSMArena lists up to three major Android upgrades for the pair.

So the real comparison is narrow. You are choosing between a flat screen and a curved one, and between two rear cameras and three.

## The telephoto camera is the whole case for the Pro

Both phones use the same 50MP main sensor, a Sony LYT700C at 1/1.56 inches with optical stabilisation. That is a good sensor for this tier, and it is identical on both, so daylight photos and general video should come from the same hardware.

The Pro's extra is a dedicated 50MP telephoto camera at 70mm equivalent, with 3x optical zoom and OIS. On the standard Camon 50, zooming past the main lens means cropping into the sensor. On the Pro, a 3x shot uses a dedicated lens.

That matters for a specific kind of person: someone shooting events, stage performances, street scenes, or portraits where you want compression and a little distance. It matters far less if most of your photos are food, selfies and group shots at arm's length.

## Curved or flat: which is better?

Same panel size, same resolution, same refresh rate. The difference is the edge.

A curved display looks more premium and feels slimmer in the hand. A flat one is easier to protect with a case and screen protector, and it avoids the accidental edge touches that sometimes come with curved glass. In Nigeria, where most people put a case on within a week of buying, the flat Camon 50 is not a lesser choice. For some people it is the more practical one.

## Battery and charging: no difference

This is where people expect the Pro to win, and it does not. Both carry 6,150mAh with 45W wired charging on the 4G models' official TECNO Nigeria and GSMArena listings. Neither supports wireless charging on the published spec sheets. If that is a requirement, our list of [Motorola phones that support wireless charging](https://www.revibyte.blog/motorola/which-motorola-phones-support-wireless-charging/) is the better place to shop.

One caution: retailer listings are not fully consistent. The standard Camon 50 is listed at 6,150mAh, but some Pro listings in Nigeria show 6,500mAh and a 50MP selfie camera, which are figures from the Camon 50 Ultra 5G's global listings. TECNO Nigeria's own technical specification page for the Pro lists 6,150mAh and a 32MP front camera. TECNO also notes that battery capacity can vary by region, so confirm the exact spec and model code for the unit you are buying.

## Don't confuse the Camon 50 Pro with the Camon 50 Ultra 5G

The Camon 50 Pro in this comparison is a 4G phone. The Camon 50 Ultra 5G is a separate model: it uses a Dimensity 7400 Ultimate chipset, supports 5G, and has a 50MP front camera alongside its 3x telephoto camera. On paper, its 4nm Dimensity 7400 Ultimate is a higher-tier chipset than the 6nm Helio G200 Ultimate in the 4G Camon 50 and Camon 50 Pro, so the step up is performance as well as connectivity. Nigerian retailers list it under model code CN7c.

Battery figures for the Ultra vary by source. Global spec listings, including GSMArena's, give 6,500mAh, while TECNO's Nigerian promotional material cites 6,150mAh, so confirm the capacity on the unit you are buying. Outside Nigeria, some markets sell the same hardware as the "Camon 50 Pro 5G," which is one reason retailer listings get muddled.

I could not confirm the Ultra's availability through TECNO Nigeria's official sales channels. Some Nigerian listings use "Camon 50 Pro," 5G or 6,500mAh wording that does not match the 4G Pro, so check the model code and the box before paying. A listing that says only "Camon 50 Pro" without 4G or 5G is not giving you enough information.

## FAQ

### Does the Camon 50 Pro have a better battery than the Camon 50?

No, going by TECNO Nigeria's and GSMArena's listings, both are 6,150mAh with 45W wired charging. Some retailers show 6,500mAh for the Pro, which matches the separate Ultra 5G's global listings, so confirm the spec on your unit before paying.

### Is the Camon 50 Pro's main camera better?

On paper, it is the same. Both use a 50MP Sony LYT700C main sensor with OIS and an 8MP ultrawide. The Pro's advantage is the extra 3x telephoto lens.

### Do both phones have a curved screen?

No. The Pro is curved and the standard Camon 50 is flat. Panel size, resolution and 144Hz refresh rate are the same.

### Does either phone support 5G?

Not the 4G models covered here. The Camon 50 Ultra 5G is a separate model with a different chipset and 5G connectivity.

### Are they waterproof?

Both are listed with IP68, IP69 and IP69K ratings. Those cover dust and water, but a rating is not a guarantee against every kind of damage.

### Is the Camon 50 Pro worth the extra money?

Only if you will use 3x optical zoom or want the curved display. If not, the Camon 50 delivers nearly everything else for less.

### How long will these phones get software updates?

Spec listings mention up to three major Android upgrades for both, which covers new Android versions. Security patches are a separate matter, and I did not find a clear patch-length commitment for the Nigerian models, so confirm with TECNO or Carlcare before relying on a number.
