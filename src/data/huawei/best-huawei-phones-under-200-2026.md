## Overview 

Huawei doesn't dominate headlines in Nigeria the way it used to, but its budget Y series and entry-level Nova phones can still be found at Computer Village and on Jumia and Jiji — usually at prices that make Xiaomi and Infinix look expensive by comparison. Here's the honest framing though: every phone on this list launched between 2020 and 2023. Huawei's budget-phone selection available to Nigerian buyers has become much less current since the US trade restrictions began in 2019, with older models making up much of the sub-$200 stock you'll encounter. So this guide isn't "the newest Huawei phones" — it's the Huawei phones from that older lineup that are still realistically worth buying under $200 in 2026, alongside the ones that probably aren't anymore.  
  
Naira pricing swings enough with exchange rates and parallel-import costs that we're not quoting fixed figures here — check current listings on Jumia, Jiji, or with Computer Village dealers and cross-reference at least two sellers before buying.  
  
## Before you buy: the Google Play Services problem  
  
This matters more than any spec on this page. Every phone below runs EMUI without Google Mobile Services, a direct result of the 2019 US trade restrictions. There's no Play Store out of the box. You'll be installing apps through Huawei AppGallery, or sideloading via Aurora Store or APKPure. Most everyday apps, including WhatsApp, work fine once installed this way — but some banking apps, Google-dependent apps, and games with strict anti-cheat or security checks may not work at all. If that's a dealbreaker for your banking app or workflow, budget phones from other brands will save you the hassle.

## 1. Huawei Nova Y71 — best overall  
  
Released in mid-2023, the Nova Y71 is the newest phone on this list and it shows. You get a 6.75-inch HD+ display, 8GB of RAM as standard, 128GB of storage, and a 6,000mAh battery — genuinely large for this price bracket, useful if you tend to go long stretches without a charger. Its 48MP main camera is paired with 5MP ultrawide and 2MP depth cameras. 22.5W charging helps offset the bigger battery's charge time. It sits closer to the $200 ceiling than the rest here, so confirm current pricing carefully, but if you want the newest hardware among the Huawei phones on this list, this is it.  
  
Who it's for: buyers who want the most capable phone here and don't mind stretching the budget.

## 2. Huawei Y7a — best display sharpness  
  
The Y7a's 6.67-inch Full HD+ display is a real step up in pixel density from the HD+ panels on most of the other phones here, and its 48MP main camera (paired with ultrawide and macro sensors) gives it strong resolution for the price. It runs Huawei's own Kirin 710A chipset with 4GB of RAM, and the 5,000mAh battery supports 22.5W fast charging — quick for this price bracket. The trade-off: it launched in October 2020, so the chipset is dated by 2026 standards. It handles everyday apps and social media fine; heavier mobile games will need lowered settings.  
  
Who it's for: buyers who prioritize screen quality and camera resolution over raw processing speed.  
  
![Huawei Y7a Crush Green official](/assets/huawei/huawei-y7a-crush-green.jpg)
  
## 3. Huawei Nova Y61 — best 50MP option, but check the variant carefully  
  
The Nova Y61 was announced in October 2022 with a 50MP main camera — higher-resolution on paper than the 48MP setup on the Y7a — plus USB-C charging at 22.5W and a 6.52-inch HD+ display at a standard 60Hz refresh rate. This is the one to be careful with: Huawei sells closely related devices under the Nova Y61, Nova Y62, and Nova Y62 Plus names, and memory configurations differ by region — some listings show 4GB/64GB, others 6GB/64GB, and higher-spec variants go up to 8GB/128GB. Don't assume the RAM or storage you see advertised applies to every unit sold under this name; confirm the exact model number and specs sheet for the specific phone in front of you before paying. The commonly listed variants use a 5,000mAh battery with 22.5W charging, which should comfortably handle a full day for most moderate users, though actual endurance depends on network conditions, screen brightness, and how you use it.  
  
Who it's for: buyers who want a newer Huawei model with a 50MP main camera and are willing to double-check variant specs before buying.

## 4. Huawei Y6p — cheapest phone with a big battery  
  
The Y6p pairs a 6.3-inch HD+ display with a 5,000mAh battery, 3-4GB of RAM, and 64GB of storage on a MediaTek Helio P22 chipset. The triple rear camera (13MP main, 5MP ultrawide, 2MP depth) is a step up from the Y5p and handles daylight photography reasonably well for its class. The biggest reason to consider the Y6p in 2026 is its 5,000mAh battery and potentially low used price — not its performance. It launched in 2020 on a budget chipset that was modest even then, so if raw speed or a sharper display matters to you, the Y7a or Nova Y61 are better picks.  
  
Who it's for: buyers who want the biggest battery-per-naira and don't need camera or display upgrades.  
  
![Huawei Y6p ](/assets/huawei/huawei-y6p-official.jpg)
  
## 5. Huawei Y5p — only for the tightest budgets  
  
The Y5p is Huawei's cheapest recent phone, and in 2026 it's genuinely hard to recommend outside of very tight budgets. A 5.45-inch HD+ display, 2GB of RAM, 32GB of storage, and a single 8MP rear camera were modest even at launch in 2020; by current standards, 2GB of RAM will struggle to keep more than a couple of apps open comfortably. The 3,020mAh battery is also the smallest here by a wide margin. It still functions as a basic calling-and-messaging device, and if it's priced dramatically lower than everything else on this list, that's the only scenario where it makes sense.  
  
Who it's for: an absolute first phone, or a backup device, where price is the only deciding factor.

## Where to buy in Nigeria, and what to check  
  
Computer Village in Lagos is one of the best-known places to physically compare these phones and negotiate, especially for parallel-import units. Online, Jumia and Jiji both list most of these models from third-party sellers — Jiji tends to carry more used stock, which can work in your favor price-wise if you're comfortable buying pre-owned.  
  
Whatever route you take, check the exact model number before paying, not just the marketing name. Regional variants of the same phone — as with the Nova Y61/Y62 family above — can ship with different LTE band support, and a unit built for a different region may not connect properly to MTN or Airtel's 4G bands in Nigeria. Ask the seller for the model number printed on the box or in the phone's settings, search it against the official specification sheet, and confirm the LTE bands listed match what's used locally before you commit — especially with older or used stock where the origin isn't always clear.

## FAQ  
  
**Do Huawei phones under $200 have Google Play Store?**  
No. Every phone on this list runs EMUI without Google Mobile Services. You'll install apps through Huawei AppGallery, or sideload through Aurora Store or APKPure. Most major apps, including WhatsApp, still work once installed this way, but some banking apps and security-strict games may not.  
  
**Which is the best Huawei phone under $200 for gaming?**  
None of these are built for serious gaming — they all use budget chipsets (Helio P22, Kirin 710/710A) designed for efficiency over performance. The Nova Y71 and Y7a handle lighter titles like eFootball or casual games acceptably, but expect to lower graphics settings.  
  
**Is it safe to buy a used Huawei phone from Jiji or Computer Village?**  
It can be, if you check the IMEI status and confirm the exact model number before paying, and test the phone in person where possible. Huawei's official Nigeria support options have narrowed in recent years, so buying used carries more risk than with brands that still have full local warranty support — factor that into your price negotiation.  
  
**How much are these phones in naira?**  
Prices fluctuate with the exchange rate, import costs, and whether a unit is new, used, or a parallel import — naira listings for the same phone can vary by more than double between sellers. Always confirm the current rate and cross-check listed prices across at least two sellers before buying.  
  
**Can I use a Huawei phone under $200 with 5G?**  
No. All five phones on this list are 4G LTE only. If 5G matters to you, you're better off looking at newer budget 5G phones from brands such as Infinix, TECNO, Xiaomi, or Samsung.
