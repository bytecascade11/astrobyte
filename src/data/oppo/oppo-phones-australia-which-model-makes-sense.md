## Overview 

OPPO doesn't get talked about in Australia the way Samsung or Apple does, but it's quietly become one of the more interesting Android options here — mostly because JB Hi-Fi, Officeworks, and OPPO's own store carry the full range on launch day. As of September 2026, the current lineup runs from a $229 RRP entry phone to a $2,299 Hasselblad-branded flagship, and OPPO refreshes its Reno line often enough that it's easy to end up comparing a model that's already been replaced. Here's what's actually current, and which tier fits which buyer.

There are three series worth paying attention to right now: the A series for value, the freshly launched Reno16 series for the mid-range, and the Find X9 series at the top. OPPO also sells a foldable, the Find N6, for buyers who want something different at flagship money.

## Budget: three A-series tiers, not two

The A-series gives Australian buyers three main options at the budget end. All three are confirmed Australian retail models, listed directly on OPPO Australia's own site alongside JB Hi-Fi, Officeworks, Harvey Norman, and other local retailers — worth noting since OPPO's A-series naming varies more than most between regions. The A6x 4G runs a Snapdragon 685 with a 6,100mAh battery and no standout fast-charging spec, and sits at $229 RRP. The A6x 5G swaps in a Dimensity 6300 chipset for 5G connectivity, keeps the same 6,100mAh battery, and costs $299. Sitting above both is the A6 5G at $399, which also uses the Dimensity 6300 but adds a bigger 6,550mAh battery, 45W SUPERVOOC charging, IP69 water and dust resistance, and doubles the storage to 256GB with 6GB of RAM. OPPO Australia has also confirmed three years of major OS updates and six years of security patches for the A6 5G specifically — a genuinely strong promise for a budget phone.

If your budget tops out around $230–300 and you mainly want calls, messaging, and social apps, either A6x variant does the job. If you can stretch to $399, the A6 5G is the more sensible buy of the three — the extra RAM, storage, and water resistance are worth more than the $100 gap suggests.

Anyone weighing this segment against other brands might also want to see how [Nothing's sub-$200 phones](https://www.revibyte.blog/nothing/nothing-phones-under-200-2026/) compare, since that's the nearest price bracket competing for the same buyer.

## Mid-range: the Reno16 series is the one to look at

OPPO's Reno16 series launched in Australia on 23 July 2026, replacing the Reno15 lineup, and it's the generation to shop if you're buying new rather than clearing out old stock. The Reno16 F at $999 uses a Dimensity 7300-Energy chipset, a 6,500mAh battery with 45W SUPERVOOC charging, and a triple rear camera with a 50MP main sensor and a 50MP telephoto lens rated for 3.5x optical zoom, plus IP68, IP69, and IP69K water and dust resistance. The Reno16 Pro at $1,599 steps up to a Dimensity 8550 chip, a 200MP main sensor (the telephoto stays at 3.5x optical zoom, same as the F), a 6,000mAh battery, and 80W SUPERVOOC charging, alongside the same IP69K rating and a new AI Snap Key shortcut button shared across the series.

Older Reno13 and Reno13 Pro stock is still floating around some retailers, occasionally at a genuinely good discount, but it's a previous-generation chipset and camera setup — only worth choosing over the Reno16 series if the price gap is large enough to matter to you.

## Flagship: Find X9 and Find X9 Pro

OPPO's flagship push in Australia got noticeably more serious with the Find X9 series, which went on sale locally on 6 November 2025 through JB Hi-Fi and OPPO's own store. Both phones run the Dimensity 9500 chipset, both clear 7,000mAh in battery capacity (a genuinely large number for a flagship), and both carry IP69 water resistance plus the same customisable Snap Key shortcut button found on the Reno16 series.

The biggest difference between them is the camera system, although the Pro also brings a larger 6.78-inch display and a bigger 7,500mAh battery versus the standard Find X9's 6.59-inch screen and 7,025mAh cell. On cameras, the standard Find X9 uses a 50MP triple system across main, ultra-wide, and telephoto. The Pro brings in Hasselblad tuning and a 200MP telephoto sensor capable of 3x optical zoom, extendable further with an optional teleconverter accessory sold separately for $699. Unless zoom photography or the larger screen specifically matter to you, the regular Find X9 covers most of what the Pro offers for $500 less. OPPO has confirmed the Find X9 series for five major OS upgrades and six years of security updates, which trails what Samsung and Google now promise on their own flagships but is a step up from OPPO's own mid-range and budget tiers.

Buyers cross-shopping at this price point tend to also look at [how the Pixel 10, 10 Pro, and 10a compare](https://www.revibyte.blog/pixel/google-pixel-10-vs-10-pro-vs-10a/), since that trio sits in roughly the same bracket with a very different camera approach.

## What about the foldable?

OPPO also sells the Find N6, its current book-style foldable, at $3,299 through its own store — well above the rest of the lineup and aimed squarely at buyers who specifically want a folding screen rather than the best value per dollar. It's not really a competing option to anything else on this list; if a foldable is the format you want, it's worth researching on its own terms rather than comparing it directly against the Find X9 Pro.

## So which one actually makes sense?

| If you're this kind of buyer... | Go with... |
|---|---|
| Tight budget, mainly calls/texts/social apps | OPPO A6x 4G or 5G |
| Want the best all-round budget phone | OPPO A6 5G |
| Upgrading an old phone, want current-gen reliability | OPPO Reno16 F 5G |
| Care about camera zoom in the mid-range | OPPO Reno16 Pro 5G |
| Want flagship power, don't need the best zoom | OPPO Find X9 |
| Camera is the priority, budget isn't | OPPO Find X9 Pro |

For buyers who want a current Reno without jumping to flagship pricing, the Reno16 F is the most sensible starting point — it's current-generation hardware, including the same 3.5x optical zoom telephoto lens as the Pro, without the Pro-level premium. If camera resolution and processing power matter more to you than price, the Reno16 Pro is the more compelling upgrade, and the gap from there up to the Find X series is hard to justify unless flagship-level performance or zoom specifically matters.

## FAQ

**Is OPPO a reliable brand to buy in Australia?**
Yes — OPPO sells through official channels including JB Hi-Fi, Officeworks, Harvey Norman, and its own local online store, with an Australian Consumer Law-backed warranty on locally purchased devices. Grey-import units bought from overseas sellers typically aren't covered under OPPO Australia's warranty, so it's worth confirming the retailer is an authorised Australian seller.

**What's the actual difference between the Find X9 and Find X9 Pro?**
Mostly the camera and battery capacity. The Pro adds Hasselblad tuning, a 200MP telephoto sensor, a larger screen, and a bigger battery, for roughly $500 more.

**Does OPPO offer 5G on its cheaper phones?**
Yes, but check the exact model name carefully. The A6x comes in both 4G-only and 5G variants that look nearly identical apart from the chipset, so it's easy to buy the wrong one by accident. The A6 5G is a separate, higher-tier model again.

**How many years of software updates do OPPO phones get?**
It varies by series and tier. The Find X9 series is rated for five years of major OS upgrades and six years of security updates, while the budget A6 5G is rated for three years of OS updates and six years of security patches — check the specific model rather than assuming one policy covers the whole range.

**Is the Reno16 Pro worth the jump over the Reno16 F?**
Mainly if the extra camera resolution and processing power matter to you. Both share the same 3.5x optical zoom telephoto lens and the same IP69K rating, so the Pro's $600 premium is largely paying for the 200MP main sensor, the Dimensity 8550 chip, and a bigger battery rather than a completely different camera experience.
