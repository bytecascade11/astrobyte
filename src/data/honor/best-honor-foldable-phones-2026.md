## Overview 

I keep getting the same question from readers: "which Honor fold should I actually buy?" It's a fair one. Honor isn't as loud as Samsung in this market, but its foldables have been genuinely competitive for a couple of years now, and the gap became a lot harder to ignore once the Magic V6 landed in 2026.

Honor's current lineup goes back further than most buyers realize — the Magic V3 is still officially listed on Honor's global site alongside newer models. For most Nigerian buyers, though, these are the three Honor foldables I'd focus on right now: one is the newest flagship, one is last year's model that's now priced more sensibly, and one is an older option that only makes sense if you find it cheap.

**Our picks:**
- **Best overall:** Honor Magic V6
- **Best value:** Honor Magic V5
- **Best cheap used option:** Honor Magic Vs3

## Honor Magic V6 — the one to buy if budget isn't the deciding factor

The Magic V6 announced in China in March 2026, with global availability following from June through July across markets like Malaysia, Singapore, the UK, and parts of Europe. It's Honor's newest book-style fold, and on paper, the battery is the standout: a 6,510mAh rated cell (6,660mAh typical capacity) inside a phone that's still under 9mm folded is not a small engineering feat, and reviewers who've spent time with it have consistently flagged all-day endurance as one of its strongest traits for a foldable this size.

It also ships with the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5, IP68/IP69 water and dust resistance (rare for a foldable), 80W wired and 66W wireless charging, and a triple camera setup led by a 50MP main sensor with a 64MP periscope telephoto lens. MagicOS 10 leans heavily into on-device AI features, some genuinely useful, some clearly there to tick a spec-sheet box.

The catch is price. Officially it starts north of $1,600 depending on region and configuration, and there's no dedicated Nigerian retail channel for it yet, so importing one means paying a real premium plus shipping and, often, losing standard warranty coverage.

## Honor Magic V5 — the smarter buy for most people

![Honor Magic V5 held in hand showing its slim folded profile](/assets/honor/honor-magic-v5-profile.jpg)

This is the one I'd point most readers toward. The Magic V5 held the title of world's slimmest foldable when it launched, and even next to the newer V6, it doesn't feel dated. It runs the Snapdragon 8 Elite rather than the V6's newer Gen 5 chip, so there's a real generational gap in raw silicon, but the difference is unlikely to be noticeable outside of benchmarks for most everyday use. The battery is still large by foldable standards, and the display holds up well for split-screen work and video.

What makes it the pragmatic pick is availability and pricing. It's the Honor fold you're most likely to actually find listed on Jumia Nigeria or from established Computer Village dealers, and because the V6 has taken over as the flagship, V5 pricing has softened. Local listings I checked in August 2026 ranged anywhere from roughly ₦600,000 for used units up to well over ₦2 million for brand-new sealed models with full warranty — these are observed market ranges rather than fixed prices, so shop around, check current listings yourself, and confirm IMEI and warranty status before paying anything.

If you want the deeper dive on how it holds up specifically on battery life, [our dedicated battery-life comparison](https://www.revibyte.blog/honor/best-honor-phones-battery-life-2026/) covers that in detail, and if charging speed matters more to you than raw capacity, [our fast-charging roundup](https://www.revibyte.blog/honor/best-honor-phones-fast-charging-2026/) is worth a read too.

## Honor Magic Vs3 — only if the price is genuinely low

The Vs3 is a 2024 device at this point. It runs older hardware and is noticeably thicker than the V5 and V6, so its appeal now depends heavily on price. I wouldn't recommend seeking one out new, but if you come across a well-priced used unit with a clean screen and no hinge play, it's still a capable foldable for basic multitasking and note-taking. Just don't pay anywhere close to Magic V5 pricing for it.

## Buying a Honor foldable in Nigeria: what actually matters

Because these specific foldables don't currently have a dedicated Honor Nigeria retail channel, the biggest risk isn't the hardware — it's the transaction. A few things I'd insist on before paying:

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| IMEI verification | Confirms the unit isn't blacklisted or reported stolen |
| Screen crease and hinge test | Foldables show wear here first; test folding/unfolding repeatedly in person |
| Battery health check, if used | If the software exposes cycle count or battery health, check it — also watch for abnormal drain or charging behavior as a sign of a worn cell |
| Warranty documentation | Determines whether you have any recourse if something fails in month two |
| Original charger and box | Affects resale value and confirms it's not a stripped/refurbished unit |

## Which one should you buy?

If money genuinely isn't the constraint and you want the newest hardware with the best battery and water resistance, get the Magic V6. If you want much of the V6 experience for meaningfully less money and you're comfortable buying V5 stock while it's still being sold, that's the better decision for most Nigerian buyers. The Vs3 only makes sense as an opportunistic used purchase.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the Honor Magic V6 available to buy in Nigeria yet?**
There's no dedicated retail listing or launch channel for it in Nigeria, unlike markets such as the UK or Malaysia where Honor sells it directly. Most units in the country are imports, sold through Computer Village dealers or listed on platforms like Jiji.ng, which usually means a price premium and no standard local warranty.

**Is the Honor Magic V5 still worth buying now that the V6 exists?**
Yes, for most buyers. The performance gap between the two isn't large enough to justify the price gap, especially since V5 stock is more available and often better priced locally.

**Do Honor foldables get software updates as long as Samsung's?**
Honor has extended its update commitment on its newest flagship foldables, though actual rollout speed and duration can vary by region, so don't treat manufacturer promises as a guarantee for imported units.

**Are Honor foldables durable enough for daily use in Nigeria's climate?**
The Magic V6's IP68/IP69 rating is a genuine advantage for dust and humidity, something older Honor folds and many competing foldables still lack. That said, the folding mechanism itself remains the most failure-prone part of any book-style foldable regardless of brand.

**Should I buy a used Honor foldable or wait for a new one?**
If your budget is tight, a well-inspected used Magic V5 is a reasonable middle ground — just don't skip the hinge and battery health checks above. A brand-new unit with warranty is always the safer choice if you can afford it.
