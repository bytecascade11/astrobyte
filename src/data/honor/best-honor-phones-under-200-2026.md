## Overview 

Honor doesn't sell through an official Nigerian store, which is exactly why its budget lineup gets overlooked here. Most of what people know about the brand starts and stops at the Magic7 Pro or the X9d — phones that live well north of $400. But underneath that, Honor runs a genuinely deep budget catalog: the X-series and Play-series phones that China, the Middle East, and parts of Southeast Asia get to buy for under $200 before anyone else has heard of them.  
  
This list sticks to that price band on purpose. No X9-series phone made the cut — the X9d and X9c both retail closer to $350–$450, and even the "cheap" X8c settles around $250. What's here are five phones that hold a base configuration at or under $200 in at least one region, verified against listings from GSMArena and Kimovil rather than a single retailer's promo price. One exception is worth flagging up front: the Honor X7c's base 6/128GB variant starts around $194, but its 8/256GB configuration crosses to roughly $210 depending on region. It's included because the entry-level trim clears the bar — just be sure you're pricing the 6/128GB model if staying strictly under $200 matters to you.

## How we picked these  
  
Every phone below had to clear three filters: a documented base-configuration retail price at or under $200 in at least one market, a chipset and battery combination that's actually usable in 2026 (no phones stuck on a single-core-starved SoC or a battery under 5,000mAh), and specs cross-checked against at least two independent listings, since Honor's regional variants genuinely differ — the same model number can ship with different specs depending on where it's sold, and not every source agrees on which is correct. Where sources disagreed, we've flagged it below rather than picked the more flattering number.

![Honor X7c smartphone](/assets/honor/honor-x7c-2026.jpg)

Stick to the 6/128GB configuration if staying under $200 is a hard requirement — the 8/256GB trim runs closer to $210.

Honor has no official retail presence in Nigeria as of this writing, so every phone on this list would come in through grey-market importers or platforms like Jumia and Konga's third-party sellers. A few things worth checking before paying: confirm the unit is Global ROM (not a China-firmware model that defaults to Mandarin and lacks Google services out of the box), and ask what the return window is if the phone arrives faulty — grey-market imports typically don't carry Honor's own warranty in-country.  
  
Band compatibility is worth double-checking rather than assuming. Band 3 (1800MHz) is the closest thing to a universal 4G band in Nigeria at this point — MTN, Airtel, and 9mobile all deploy it, and Glo has rolled it out in major cities alongside its original Band 28 (700MHz) network, though Band 28 coverage still varies by location and isn't guaranteed on every phone. Since this can change by network and by region, it's worth confirming with the seller or your carrier's support line which bands a specific phone supports before you commit, rather than relying on a general rule.  
  
On pricing, treat any naira conversion as a rough guide rather than a fixed number. At an exchange rate of roughly ₦1,400–₦1,430 to $1 (NgnRates.com, late August 2026), a $120 X6c works out to somewhere in the ₦168,000–₦172,000 range before import costs — but grey-market pricing moves with shipping fees, importer margins, and the naira's own volatility, so expect actual street prices in Nigeria to run higher than that raw conversion and to shift over time.

## FAQ  
  
**Is the Honor X9d on this list?**  
No. The X9d retails between roughly $350 and $450 depending on configuration, well above the $200 ceiling here. It's a genuinely different tier of phone, with an 8,300mAh battery and a Snapdragon 6 Gen 4 chipset.  
  
**Do any of these phones support 5G?**  
None of the five models listed here gives you a straightforward 5G buying option. The Play 60 uses a Dimensity 6300, a chipset that supports 5G in other devices, but the configuration sold here runs on 4G only. For an actual 5G Honor phone, you'd need to move up to the X8c or X9-series, both of which sit above the $200 mark.  
  
**Which one has the best camera?**  
The Honor X7c has the strongest camera hardware on this list — its 108MP main camera outresolves the 50MP setups on the X6c, X6b, and X5c Plus, and the Play 60's single 13MP camera. That said, megapixel count alone doesn't determine real-world image quality; sensor size, processing, and optics all play a role too, and none of these phones has been independently camera-tested by us at the time of writing.  
  
**Are these phones officially sold in Nigeria?**  
No. Honor doesn't run an official retail channel in Nigeria, so all five phones here would be grey-market imports. See the buying-cautions section above before ordering one.  
  
**Is battery life good across this lineup?**  
Generally yes — every phone here ships with at least 5,200mAh, and the Play 60 and higher-capacity X7c variant both reach 6,000mAh. Charging speed varies more than capacity does, ranging from 15W on the X5c Plus and Play 60 up to 35W on the X6c, X6b, and X7c.
