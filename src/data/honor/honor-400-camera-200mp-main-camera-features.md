## The 200MP main camera, without the hype

The HONOR 400 uses a 200MP main sensor with a 1/1.4-inch sensor size and optical image stabilization. Sensor size can matter more than megapixel count when it comes to light gathering and low-light performance. A bigger sensor collects more light, and more light is what separates a clean night shot from a smeared one.

Here is the part most spec sheets skip. The phone doesn't normally save 200MP images. HONOR says you need to enter High-Res mode to activate the full 200MP resolution, and that the actual photo resolution varies by shooting mode.

The reason is a general imaging principle called pixel binning. Sensors with this many tiny pixels often combine groups of them into one larger effective pixel, which usually gives cleaner colour and less noise than shooting every pixel separately. High-Res mode gives up that benefit for extra detail, so it makes the most sense in bright daylight, where you plan to crop or print.

That has a practical side too. A full-resolution 200MP shot can produce a much larger file than the phone's normal photo output. If you shoot in that mode a lot, your storage will disappear fast, and we covered how to think about that in our guide on [128GB vs 256GB storage](https://www.revibyte.blog/motorola/motorola-128gb-vs-256gb-storage/). For this phone, 256GB is the safer pick if the camera is why you're buying it.

## What it does well

**Daylight detail.** In good light, the main camera produces sharp images with enough room to crop in. Zooming into a distant shopfront or a street sign still leaves you with something usable, which cheaper phones cannot say.

**Low light.** The larger sensor and OIS give the main camera a solid foundation for low-light photography. Independent testing, such as GSMArena's, found the results good, though close inspection can reveal noise and processing artifacts. On a phone screen it holds up well. Pixel-peeping is where it shows.

**Selfies.** The 50MP front camera is one of the stronger parts of the setup, producing detailed selfies in good and low light. The lens is wide enough for group shots, and skin tones look more natural if you leave the beauty filters off.

## The AI camera features

HONOR leans hard on software with this phone, and some of it is actually useful.

| Feature | What it does |
|---|---|
| AI Erase | Removes people or objects that ruin a photo |
| AI Outpainting | Extends the frame when you framed the shot too tight |
| AI Image to Video | Generates a short video from a still image |
| AI Remove Reflection | Reduces reflections from glass and windows |
| AI Upscale | Improves the detail of lower-resolution images |
| AI Cutout | Separates a subject from its background |

The eraser is likely the one you will reach for most. Tools like this usually work well on simple backgrounds and get messy on busy ones. Image to Video is the flashiest addition, but availability and pricing depend on your market and service. HONOR's UK page, for example, lists it as a paid feature, so confirm what applies in Nigeria before counting on it. Treat the AI tools as a bonus, not the reason to buy.

If you want to see how hardware-side camera thinking compares, look at what we wrote on the [iPhone 18 Pro's variable aperture camera](https://www.revibyte.blog/apple/iphone-18-pro-variable-aperture-camera-review/). Apple changes the lens itself. HONOR mostly changes the result after the shutter.

## Where it falls short

The ultra-wide is the weak link. At 12MP it is fine outdoors in daylight, but it loses detail at the edges and struggles once the light drops. If you shoot a lot of wide scenes at night, expect softness.

There is also no dedicated telephoto lens on the standard HONOR 400. Its shorter-range zoom relies heavily on the high-resolution main sensor, while longer 15x to 30x levels use HONOR's AI Super Zoom processing. That is software reconstructing detail, not optics, so treat the far end as a tool for identifying something distant rather than for your best photos. If you want a real telephoto lens, the Pro version is the one to look at.

Video is decent, not class-leading. Stabilization keeps walking shots watchable, but it will not match a flagship in tricky light.

## How it compares to other camera phones

Reviews of Google's Pixel line have long praised its processing for consistent, point-and-shoot results, and our look at the [Pixel 11 camera](https://www.revibyte.blog/pixel/google-pixel-11-camera-everything-new-2026/) covers how that approach continues. The two phones simply prioritise different things. Pixel leans on processing consistency. The HONOR 400 leans on a high-resolution main sensor and the cropping flexibility that comes with it. If you want reliable results with little effort, look at Pixel. If you want more detail to play with, HONOR makes a stronger case.

## FAQ

### Does the HONOR 400 really shoot 200MP photos?

Yes, but not by default. HONOR says you need to enter High-Res mode to activate the full 200MP resolution, and that actual photo resolution varies by shooting mode.

### Is 200MP better than 50MP?

Not automatically. Sensor size and processing matter more. High-Res mode helps when you crop heavily or print large, while lower-resolution output is usually the better fit for everyday shots.

### Does the HONOR 400 have optical image stabilization?

The main camera does, which helps with night shots and handheld video.

### Does the HONOR 400 have a telephoto lens?

The standard model does not. Shorter-range zoom relies on the high-resolution main sensor, and HONOR's AI Super Zoom handles the 15x to 30x levels. The Pro model adds a dedicated telephoto.

### How good is the front camera?

The 50MP selfie camera is one of the stronger parts of the setup, producing detailed selfies in good and low light, with a view wide enough for group photos.

### Is the HONOR 400 good for video?

It is good enough for social content and everyday recording. Serious videographers will want something with stronger stabilization and more control.

### How much storage do I need?

If you plan to use the 200MP mode often, go for 256GB. The files are large and add up quickly.

## Final thoughts

The HONOR 400 does not need the 200MP label to be a good camera phone, which is a good sign. The sensor is big, the stabilization is solid, and the selfie camera is strong. The ultra-wide and the lack of real zoom keep it from being an easy recommendation for everyone.

If your priorities are clear, daylight quality, selfies and value, it is easy to like.
