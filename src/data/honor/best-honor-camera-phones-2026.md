## Overview 

Honor spent 2026 doing two things at once: chasing periscope resolution numbers that sound made up, and testing whether a camera bolted to a motorized arm is a gimmick or a genuine format shift — a bet it followed through on, launching the Robot Phone in China on August 12. Between those extremes sit five phones that deliver on the "camera phone" promise rather than just printing a big megapixel figure on the spec sheet.  
  
**How this list was put together:** ranking is based on camera hardware (sensor size, telephoto reach, stabilization), independent testing where it exists (DXOMARK, GSMArena's lab reviews), confirmed pricing versus estimated pricing, and whether a phone is realistically obtainable in Nigeria through Jumia, Slot, or Honor's own regional storefronts rather than grey-market import only. Specs below are sourced from GSMArena's hardware breakdowns and Honor's own regional spec pages; anything still unconfirmed — sensor model numbers Honor hasn't officially disclosed, for instance — is flagged as such rather than stated as fact.

## 1. Honor Magic8 Pro — the all-round flagship pick  
  
The Magic8 Pro is Honor's most complete camera phone this year, and it doesn't try to reinvent what already worked on the Magic7 Pro. The triple system pairs a 50MP main sensor (1/1.3-inch class, exact sensor model not officially disclosed by Honor) with a 50MP ultrawide and a 200MP periscope telephoto built around a Samsung ISOCELL HP9 sensor at f/2.6, giving roughly 3.7x optical zoom before digital cropping kicks in. DXOMARK's independent testing places the zoom and low-light performance among the strongest in Honor's current lineup, and the confirmed Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 chipset keeps processing snappy for RAW-heavy workflows.  
  
One region-specific detail worth knowing before ordering: battery capacity differs by market. GSMArena reports 7,200mAh for the Chinese model, while Honor's global specification page lists 7,100mAh.  
  
**Nigeria availability:** listed through Jumia Nigeria's official storefront. Third-party retail listings for this model vary widely (roughly ₦370,000–₦1.2 million depending on source and date), which likely reflects a mix of storage tiers, timing, and unofficial resellers rather than one stable price — check Jumia or an Honor-authorized dealer directly for current pricing.  
  
## 2. Honor 600 Pro — high-end camera hardware at a mid-range price  
  
If the Magic8 Pro's price tag is the problem, the 600 Pro is Honor's answer. GSMArena's confirmed spec breakdown lists a 200MP main camera (1/1.4-inch sensor, f/1.9, OIS) alongside a dedicated 50MP telephoto at f/2.8 with 3.5x optical zoom, a 12MP autofocusing ultrawide, and a 7,000mAh battery (6,400mAh in the European variant) on the Snapdragon 8 Elite chipset. Honor's CIPA 6.0/6.5 stabilization certification claims on the main and telephoto cameras respectively are worth noting but haven't been independently re-verified against the CIPA standard by a third party in the sources checked for this piece.  
  
For buyers who want most of the low-light capability of the numbered flagships without the periscope price premium, this is the more defensible pick — and it's confirmed on sale in Nigeria.  
  
**Nigeria availability:** listed at approximately ₦1,700,000 through Slot/authorized Honor retail as of mid-2026 — treat this as a snapshot rather than a fixed price, since retailer pricing shifts with exchange rates.

## 3. Honor Magic7 Pro — last year's flagship, still holding up  
  
The Magic7 Pro retains the same broad camera philosophy as the Magic8 Pro — a high-resolution main camera, an ultrawide, and a 200MP periscope telephoto — but it isn't a rehash with a new label. GSMArena's teardown lists a 50MP dual-aperture (f/1.4–f/2.0) main sensor at 1/1.3-inch, a 50MP ultrawide, and a 200MP telephoto built on a Samsung ISOCELL HP3-class sensor. Sources disagree somewhat on the exact optical zoom multiplier — GSMArena's news coverage cites a 60mm-equivalent lens for roughly 2.5x, while DXOMARK's camera test describes the same lens as offering 3.5x optical zoom — so treat the precise number as approximate rather than settled, though the hardware itself is consistently reported.  
  
DXOMARK's retest found daylight zoom detail and color accuracy solid, but noted a more noticeable drop in low-light zoom quality compared with category leaders like the Oppo Find X8 Ultra and Xiaomi 15 Ultra. Reviewers have also flagged the telephoto's default output as occasionally soft, though selfie quality is a genuine strength. Retail pricing has settled meaningfully below the Magic8 Pro's, making this the value pick for anyone chasing periscope zoom without current-flagship pricing — and it's a phone with a real secondhand and current-retail presence in Nigeria, unlike some newer releases.  
  
**Estimated price:** roughly $890–$1,100 on international marketplace listings (GSMArena, August 2026); Nigerian retail listings exist on Jiji but weren't consistent enough across sources to quote a single Naira figure with confidence.  
  
## 4. Honor Robot Phone — the experimental one, and it's real now  
  
This is the phone that doesn't fit the usual "which camera phone should I buy" framing. Honor launched it in Guangzhou on August 12, 2026, confirming the hardware that had circulated in leaks for months: a 200MP main sensor (1/1.28-inch, f/1.6) mounted on a four-DoF titanium gimbal system with three-axis mechanical stabilization — Honor's "Titanium Agile Gimbal" — plus a 50MP ultrawide and a second 200MP periscope telephoto (1/1.4-inch sensor, f/2.6, OIS, 2.7x optical zoom and up to 200x digital zoom per Honor's official spec page), developed with input from cinema equipment maker ARRI on the imaging pipeline. Honor also referenced an in-house imaging chip (Yuguang H1) at an earlier press event, though this detail has had less independent verification than the confirmed camera hardware. Honor's global spec page and GSMArena's post-launch listing both put the battery at 7,060mAh on the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5.  
  
The catch: announced retail availability is currently China-only, with pre-orders opening August 12 and retail sales starting August 18. Pricing is confirmed for China — ¥9,999 (roughly $1,375–$1,485 depending on the exchange rate used) for the 12GB/512GB model, ¥12,999 (roughly $1,790–$1,930) for 16GB/1TB — but Honor hasn't announced a global release date, international pricing, or Nigeria availability. This is one to watch rather than one to order today unless you're buying through a China-import channel.  
  
**Price:** ¥9,999 / ¥12,999 in China; no confirmed global price or Nigeria availability as of this writing

## 5. Honor 600 — the budget-conscious camera phone  
  
The standard Honor 600 trims the telephoto ambitions of the Pro model but keeps the headline 200MP main camera (1/1.4-inch sensor, same class as the Pro's, though GSMArena couldn't confirm whether it's the identical part) and matches the Pro's 7,000mAh battery, launched alongside it in April 2026. It runs on the Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 rather than the Pro's Snapdragon 8 Elite, and swaps the Pro's dedicated telephoto for digital zoom only — GSMArena's testing notes the main camera holds up reasonably to around 2x before quality drops off. A separate Lite variant exists further down the range with a 108MP main sensor and MediaTek silicon, but it's a battery-and-price play rather than a camera one — skip it if photography is the priority.  
  
For buyers who want the "big sensor" main-camera experience without paying for periscope hardware they may never use, the base 600 is the sensible entry point into Honor's 2026 camera lineup.  
  
**Estimated price:** regional launch pricing suggested roughly $430–$480 (AED 1,599 converted, April 2026); a specific, currently reliable Nigerian retail price wasn't confirmed for this piece — check Jumia or Slot directly.

## FAQ  
  
**Is the Honor Magic8 Pro's telephoto actually better than the Magic7 Pro's?**  
The hardware is close enough that most people won't notice a dramatic jump — both use a 200MP-class periscope sensor. DXOMARK's testing puts the newer model slightly ahead on low-light zoom, but the difference is incremental rather than generational, and the Magic7 Pro's telephoto sensor generation and image processing pipeline aren't identical to the Magic8 Pro's despite the shared resolution figure.  
  
**Does the Honor Robot Phone's gimbal camera actually improve photo quality, or is it a gimmick?**  
It's genuinely unclear. The hardware — a large 1/1.28-inch main sensor and an ARRI-informed imaging pipeline — is real and confirmed at launch, but a moving physical camera arm is untested territory for handheld photography, and independent hands-on reviews from outlets that have actually used the retail unit hadn't converged on a verdict at the time of writing.  
  
**Which Honor phone gives the best camera value for the price?**  
The Honor 600 Pro is the strongest balance for most buyers — a high-end main camera and dedicated telephoto at roughly half the retail price of the Magic8 Pro in Nigeria. A 200MP sensor doesn't automatically mean flagship-level image quality; processing, lens quality, and stabilization matter just as much, but the 600 Pro's confirmed hardware and CIPA-rated stabilization make a strong case for the price. The Magic7 Pro is the better pick specifically for buyers who want periscope zoom without current-flagship pricing and don't mind buying a phone that's a generation old.  
  
**Can I actually buy these in Nigeria, or is this a global-market list?**  
The Magic8 Pro and 600 Pro both have a confirmed retail presence in Nigeria (Jumia and Slot, respectively). The Magic7 Pro shows up on secondary marketplaces like Jiji. The Robot Phone is currently available for retail sale in China, with no confirmed Nigeria or broader international release date.  
  
**Is the Honor 600 (non-Pro) worth buying over the Pro model?**  
Only if the telephoto camera isn't a priority. The standard 600 keeps the 200MP main sensor and matches the Pro's battery capacity at a noticeably lower price, but loses the dedicated telephoto — digital zoom fills the gap, with the expected drop in detail past roughly 2x.
