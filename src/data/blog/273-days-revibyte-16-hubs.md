## Overview 

It's been 273 days since ReviByte [left Blogger behind and relaunched on Astro](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/first-post-revibyte-live/) on December 15, 2025. That's long enough to look back and actually see the shape of what's been built — not just individual posts, but real infrastructure.

For the full trail of how this happened in stages, earlier milestones covered [400+ daily visitors at Day 181](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/revibyte-181-days-milestone/), a [100 PageSpeed score fixed entirely from a phone](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/how-i-got-100-pagespeed-score-astro/), and [retiring the downloader tools at Day 242](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/revibyte-242-days-milestone/). This post picks up from there.

## The hubs: 16 and growing

ReviByte now runs **12 dedicated tech/device hubs** — Oppo, Samsung, Xiaomi, Tecno, Motorola, Huawei, Honor, OnePlus, Pixel, Nothing, Apple, and Vivo — each its own Astro content collection with a hub index, dynamic slug pages, breadcrumbs, schema markup, view counters, and affiliate carousels. Every post in these hubs still surfaces on the homepage feed, so nothing gets buried behind a brand URL.

Alongside that sits **4 gaming hubs**: Call of Duty Mobile, eFootball, PUBG Mobile, and Mobile Legends: Bang Bang. COD Mobile in particular has turned into the strongest pull for Tier-1 US traffic.

Getting here wasn't a straight line — a Free Fire hub was scrapped entirely after it refused to get indexed, and an itel post hit the same wall months later. Chasing that down turned up a real bug: the image sitemap generator simply never queried the itel, Samsung, or Xiaomi collections. It's a good reminder that a hub can be fully built and still invisible to Google if one file quietly excludes it.

## The tools actually work now

The old `/save/` hub — TikTok and Instagram downloaders — was [shut down proactively](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/revibyte-242-days-milestone/) after I decided the model carried too much long-term search and sustainability risk. There was no manual action; it was simply a decision to move in a safer direction.

In its place, **ReviByte Tools** now ships three working, self-hosted utilities: an Image Compressor, a Battery Health / Charging Time Estimator, and a QR Code Generator — all running client-side, no uploads, no third-party API dependency. A PDF Compressor and a phone comparison tool are next.

## The traffic curve

Daily visitors have grown from the low hundreds at launch to a steady **800–1,000+ a day** over the past several weeks, with one single post — a best-Android-phones roundup — briefly doubling that overnight. The audience skews global, with strong representation from Nigeria, the US, and Indonesia, and around 98% of it still arrives organically through Google Search. The next marker on the board is 2,000 daily visitors.

## What's next

More device hubs are still in the pipeline, the tools suite keeps growing, and the SEO groundwork — image sitemaps, structured data, canonical URL fixes — keeps paying off in faster indexing. 273 days in, ReviByte looks less like a blog and more like a small, working product.
