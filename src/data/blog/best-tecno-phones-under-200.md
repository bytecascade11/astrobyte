## Overview 

TECNO may get less hype online than Infinix, but walk into a phone market in Nigeria and its popularity is easy to understand. The tricky part in 2026 is that "under $200" isn't a fixed Naira figure anymore. Nigeria runs two benchmark rates that matter here: the CBN's official window, which held fairly steady around ₦1,360–₦1,370/$ through the first two weeks of August 2026, and the parallel (black market) rate, which is more relevant to retail electronics pricing and moved between roughly ₦1,405 and ₦1,428/$ over the same period. I used the parallel-market range to set the ceiling, since that's the rate closer to what retailers actually factor into Naira pricing. At ₦1,400–₦1,428/$, $200 works out to somewhere between ₦280,000 and ₦285,600, so I treated **₦286,000 as the practical upper limit** for this list. Where a phone has multiple RAM/storage configurations, I only included it if at least one genuinely affordable configuration falls within that ceiling.

## 1. TECNO Spark 40 — the one most people should buy  
  
If you only read one section, make it this one. The Spark 40 is TECNO's mainstream Spark model for 2026, and it's priced to move: most listings put the 4GB/128GB variant between ₦132,500 and ₦150,000, while the higher-specced 8GB/256GB version runs closer to ₦270,000 — still under our ₦286,000 ceiling, but worth knowing before you assume every configuration is cheap. It runs a MediaTek Helio G81 chipset, which won't win benchmark contests but comfortably handles WhatsApp, eFootball 2026 on low settings, and everyday multitasking. The 6.67-inch display refreshes at 120Hz, the 50MP main camera is solid for daylight shots, and the 5,200mAh battery should comfortably cover a typical day of mixed use going by its specs — I haven't run a timed battery test on this unit myself. It also carries an IP64 rating and 1.5m drop resistance, which matters more than spec-sheet bragging rights once you've actually dropped a phone on Lagos tarmac.  
  
![TECNO Spark 40 smartphone rear panel and camera module](/assets/posts/tecno-spark-40-rear-panel.jpg)  
  
**Skip it if:** you shoot a lot in low light — the single rear camera struggles once the sun goes down, and there's no ultrawide or macro lens to fall back on.  
  
## 2. TECNO Spark Go 3 — for the tightest budget  
  
Launched in January 2026 as the successor to the Spark Go 2, this is the phone to buy if your priority is "smartphone that works" over "smartphone that impresses." Pricing depends heavily on which configuration you're looking at, so it's clearer as a table than a single range:  
  
| Configuration | Typical Nigeria price | Where it's sold |  
|---|---|---|  
| 4GB RAM / 64GB storage | ₦55,000 – ₦80,000 | Independent retailers, off-channel stock |  
| 4GB RAM / 128GB storage | ₦140,000 – ₦148,500 | Larger retailers, official Nigeria-channel stock |  
  
Confirm the exact configuration before comparing prices between sellers — the two tiers aren't interchangeable, and a listing that looks unusually cheap is almost always the 64GB base unit rather than a discount on the 128GB version. One caveat on the lower end of that range: I wasn't able to confirm from official-channel sources that every ₦55,000–₦80,000 listing is for brand-new stock rather than used or refurbished units, so if you see a price in that range, ask the seller directly before buying. TECNO's own spec sheet lists this as a 6.75-inch, 120Hz HD+ display. The Unisoc T7250 chipset and 4GB of RAM (extendable via virtual memory) are built for basics: calls, messaging, banking apps, light browsing. The 5,000mAh battery should get most users through a full day based on its capacity, though I'd treat that as a reasonable inference rather than a tested result. Don't expect it to handle demanding games like COD Mobile at anything beyond low settings.  
  
![TECNO Spark Go 3 smartphone in hand](/assets/posts/tecno-spark-go-3-hands-on.jpg)  
  
**Skip it if:** you want a single device for both daily use and mobile gaming — pair this with a Spark 40 in the household instead, and use the Go 3 for lighter tasks.

## 3. TECNO Spark 40C — best battery capacity for the money  
  
The Spark 40C trims a few things from the standard Spark 40 to hit a lower price point, but it wins on one spec that matters a lot in Nigeria: battery capacity. At 6,000mAh, it should outlast most phones on this list on paper, which is useful if your area deals with unpredictable power supply — again, that's a capacity-based estimate rather than a side-by-side runtime test. Retailer listings range from around ₦113,000 up to ₦190,000 depending on RAM and storage, comfortably under our ceiling across every configuration. The trade-off is 18W charging (noticeably slower than the Spark 40's 45W) and a single 13MP rear camera instead of the 50MP sensor on its sibling. Still IP64-rated, still 120Hz, still a genuinely comfortable everyday phone.  
  
![TECNO Spark 40C smartphone front display](/assets/posts/tecno-spark-40c-front-display.jpg)  
  
**Skip it if:** camera quality is a priority — the 13MP sensor is the weakest in this lineup.  
  
## 4. TECNO Spark 40 Pro — best display, right at the budget ceiling  
  
This is where "$200" gets tight. The listings I found cluster around ₦270,000–₦276,000, although the exact storage configuration wasn't consistently stated across sources — TECNO sells this chipset/RAM combo in both 128GB and 256GB storage, so a price this close to the ceiling is worth double-checking against the exact configuration before you buy. That cluster lands under our ₦286,000 ceiling, but with very little room if the exchange rate moves against the Naira in the meantime. What you get for that is the best screen on this list: a 6.78-inch 1.5K AMOLED panel at 144Hz, paired with MediaTek's Helio G100 Ultimate and 45W charging. The 50MP main camera and 13MP selfie camera are both a tier above the base Spark. If your budget has any flex, this is the phone that feels the least like a compromise — but check the live Naira price and exact storage tier before you commit, since it's the tightest fit on this list.  
  
![TECNO Spark 40 Pro AMOLED display close-up](/assets/posts/tecno-spark-40-pro-amoled-display.jpg)  
  
**Skip it if:** your budget is firm with no room for exchange-rate swings — the standard Spark 40 gives you much more room in the budget while still handling everyday tasks comfortably.  
  
## 5. TECNO Camon 30 4G — for buyers who care about photos  
  
TECNO's Camon line exists for people who prioritize the camera, but most current Camon models — including the newer Camon 40 — price out well above $200 in Nigeria once you factor in typical configurations and retailer markups. The Camon 30 4G is the exception, and specifically its **8GB physical RAM/256GB storage configuration** (TECNO's own listing labels this "256GB ROM + 16GB RAM," where the extra 8GB is virtual/extended RAM drawn from storage, not physical memory) — which multiple retailers list between ₦271,500 and ₦283,000, under our ceiling with some room to spare. It's the previous-generation Camon, which is exactly why it's priced where it is: a 6.78-inch AMOLED display at 120Hz, a MediaTek Helio G99 Ultimate chipset, a 50MP main camera, and a 50MP selfie camera — specs that still make it a compelling option at this price. Be careful when shopping, though — the Camon 30 also comes in 12GB RAM and 5G variants that cost significantly more (up to ₦416,500 for the 5G version), so confirm you're buying the 4G, 8GB physical RAM/256GB storage unit specifically.  
  
![TECNO Camon 30 4G smartphone AMOLED display and rear camera](/assets/posts/tecno-camon-30-4g-display-camera.jpg)  
  
**Skip it if:** you want the newest hardware — this is last year's Camon at this year's discount, which is the trade-off that makes it fit the budget at all.

## FAQ  
  
**Are these prices fixed?**  
No. The CBN's official rate held fairly steady around ₦1,360–₦1,370/$ through the first two weeks of August 2026, but the parallel (black market) rate — the one closer to what retailers actually price against — moved between roughly ₦1,405 and ₦1,428/$ over the same window. Every price in this guide is a snapshot from mid-August 2026 using that parallel-market range — always check current listings on Jumia, Slot, or your local retailer before buying.  
  
**Do any of these TECNO phones support 5G?**  
The Nigerian variants covered in this guide are all 4G-only. TECNO's 5G models — including the Camon 30 5G and various Pova and Camon "5G" variants — sit well above the $200 mark. If 5G matters to you at this budget, check the [Infinix 5G phones guide](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-infinix-5g-phones/) instead.  
  
**Which one is best for gaming — MLBB, eFootball, COD Mobile?**  
The Spark 40 Pro, with its Helio G100 Ultimate chipset, should handle Mobile Legends and eFootball 2026 most comfortably of the five based on its specs. The standard Spark 40 manages both games on medium settings. The Spark Go 3 and Spark 40C are built for everyday use rather than gaming and will likely struggle with COD Mobile at anything above low settings.  
  
**Is it better to buy new or UK-used in Nigeria?**  
UK-used units can be ₦40,000–₦80,000 cheaper, but you lose local warranty coverage. For phones already this affordable, I'd generally lean toward new units with warranty unless you're working with a trusted local seller.  
  
**TECNO or Infinix — which brand is better under $200?**  
Neither wins outright; it depends on what you need. TECNO tends to edge ahead on battery capacity and camera hardware at this price tier, while Infinix (see the [under $150](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-infinix-phones-under-150/) and [under $300](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-infinix-phones-under-300/) guides) often has an advantage on chipset performance. Compare the specific models you're choosing between rather than picking a brand on reputation alone.

### Methodology and sourcing  
  
Specifications and pricing in this guide were cross-checked against retailer listings (Jumia, Slot, Jiji.ng), GSMArena, and TECNO's own product pages as of mid-August 2026. For the exchange rate, I distinguished between the CBN's official window — which held around ₦1,360–₦1,370/$ through the first two weeks of August 2026 — and the parallel (black market) rate, which is more relevant to retail electronics pricing and moved between roughly ₦1,405 and ₦1,428/$ over the same period. I used the parallel-market range to set ₦286,000 as the practical upper limit for a $200 budget. Where a model has multiple RAM/storage or network configurations, I only recommend the specific configuration that falls within that ceiling — flagged explicitly in each phone's section rather than smoothed over — and I've excluded models (like the Camon 40 and the Camon 30 5G) whose typical configurations sit clearly above it. Naira pricing is presented as a range because retailer prices vary meaningfully by storage tier, warranty status, and daily exchange-rate movement; treat these figures as a starting point for comparison, not a guaranteed quote. This post will be updated if TECNO, the CBN, or major Nigerian retailers announce changes that shift these numbers.
