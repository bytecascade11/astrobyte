## Overview 

If you are searching for the **best phone under ₦300,000 in Nigeria in 2026**, chances are one of these three names has already come up — the **Infinix Note 60 Pro**, the **Infinix Hot 60 Pro**, and the **Tecno Spark 40 Pro**. All three come from Transsion, all three target roughly the same Nigerian buyer, and all three will try to convince you they are the best value for your money.

But they are not equal. And depending on what you actually need, one of them is going to disappoint you six months after you buy it.

I have gone through the specs, the Nigerian market pricing, and the real-world implications of each phone. No spec-sheet copying, no generic breakdown — just honest analysis for the Nigerian buyer who has worked hard for their money and cannot afford to get this decision wrong.

## First — Who Are These Phones Actually For?

Before the numbers, let us be honest about what each phone is chasing.

The **Tecno Spark 40 Pro** is for the person who wants a reliable everyday phone without overthinking it. Students, young professionals, people who need something that handles social media, banking apps, and light gaming without drama. It also targets anyone who cares about design — it is genuinely slim and looks more expensive than it costs.

The **Infinix Hot 60 Pro** is a step up — it is for someone who has done their research and wants near-flagship features without paying flagship prices. If you spend hours on TikTok, YouTube, or Call of Duty Mobile and you want that smooth display experience, this phone was built with you in mind.

The **Infinix Note 60 Pro** is in a different league. This is for the person who is tired of compromising. You want Snapdragon performance, a stunning display, a battery that outlasts your whole day, and charging fast enough to fill up in minutes — not hours. You are willing to pay more for it.

## Display — What You Will Look at Every Single Day

![Infinix Note 60 Pro vs Hot 60 Pro vs Tecno Spark 40 Pro display comparison Nigeria 2026](/assets/posts/infinix-note60pro-hot60pro-spark40pro-display-comparison-nigeria.jpg)
*Display comparison — AMOLED vs AMOLED but not all panels are equal*

The **Infinix Note 60 Pro** runs a 6.78-inch AMOLED panel at 144Hz with 4,500 nits peak brightness and 1.5K resolution. Standing outside on a bright afternoon in Lagos, you will still read your screen without squinting. Colors are vivid without looking overdone. This is a premium display experience at mid-range pricing.

The **Infinix Hot 60 Pro** also carries a 6.78-inch AMOLED at 144Hz with the same 4,500 nits brightness and 1.5K resolution. On display alone, it nearly matches the Note 60 Pro — which is one of the Hot 60 Pro's strongest selling points. You are getting a screen that would have cost significantly more two years ago.

The **Tecno Spark 40 Pro** brings a 6.78-inch AMOLED at 120Hz. Tecno has upgraded from the LCD panels in older Spark models and that is a genuine improvement. The 120Hz scrolling feels smooth and the colors look good. Where it falls slightly behind is in peak brightness and pixel density compared to the other two — outdoor visibility is decent but not at the same level.

**Display winner: Note 60 Pro and Hot 60 Pro tied**

## Performance — The Engine Under the Hood

This is where the Note 60 Pro creates real distance from the competition.

The **Infinix Note 60 Pro** runs on the **Qualcomm Snapdragon 7s Gen 4** — a proper mid-range Qualcomm chip that handles everything confidently. Gaming, heavy multitasking, running multiple apps simultaneously — it manages all of it without throttling. If you play Call of Duty Mobile seriously, this chip delivers the frame-rate consistency that actually matters in competitive matches. Qualcomm chips also tend to age better than MediaTek in this segment, which is relevant if you plan to keep this phone for three to four years. See also: [Why Mid-Range Phones Are Killing Flagships in 2026](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/why-mid-range-phones-are-killing--flagships-2026).

The **Infinix Hot 60 Pro** uses the **MediaTek Helio G200**, a capable chipset for the price. WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube, banking apps, light to moderate gaming — all handled comfortably. Where you will start feeling limits is in extended gaming sessions or serious multitasking. Not bad, but it is not the Note 60 Pro experience.

The **Tecno Spark 40 Pro** carries the **MediaTek Helio G100 Ultimate**, a step below the G200. For everyday use and casual gaming including PUBG at 60fps, it works without issues. If you push your phone hard daily, you will eventually hit the ceiling of this chip. For most everyday Nigerian users though, it is honest value.

**Performance winner: Infinix Note 60 Pro**

## Battery and Charging — The Most Important Spec in Nigeria

![Infinix Note 60 Pro 6500mAh 90W charging vs Hot 60 Pro vs Tecno Spark 40 Pro battery Nigeria](/assets/posts/infinix-note60pro-hot60pro-spark40pro-battery-charging-nigeria.jpg)
*When NEPA is unreliable, your phone battery becomes your power bank*

Nobody needs to explain to a Nigerian why battery life is not a luxury feature. NEPA has settled that debate permanently. A phone that cannot survive a full day without electricity is not a phone — it is a problem.

The **Infinix Note 60 Pro** packs a **6,500mAh battery with 90W fast charging**. That 90W speed means you can get a substantial charge in under 30 minutes. The 6,500mAh capacity means most people will reach midnight before needing to plug in. Infinix claims the battery retains over 80% capacity after 2,000 charge cycles — roughly six years of daily charging. In a market where phones are kept for three to four years on average, that long-term health claim matters. If you want to understand how Tecno phones hold up over time, check out our [Top 5 Hidden Features of the Tecno Camon 30 Pro](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/tecno-camon-30-pro-hidden-features) for a sense of how Transsion software evolves.

The **Infinix Hot 60 Pro** carries a **5,160mAh battery with 45W charging**. Decent capacity, and 45W gets you to 50% in around 22 minutes. Full-day use under normal conditions is comfortable. It is not the Note 60 Pro but it handles Nigerian daily life without leaving you stranded.

The **Tecno Spark 40 Pro** has a **5,200mAh battery with 45W charging**. Similar endurance to the Hot 60 Pro. Tecno markets the ultra-cycle battery design here, claiming it holds up well after years of heavy use — a relevant selling point for Nigerian buyers who do not upgrade phones frequently.

**Battery winner: Infinix Note 60 Pro (not close)**

## Camera — Honest Review, Not Marketing Numbers

![Infinix Note 60 Pro vs Hot 60 Pro vs Tecno Spark 40 Pro camera quality test Nigeria 2026](/assets/posts/infinix-note60pro-hot60pro-spark40pro-camera-nigeria-test.jpg)
*All three have 50MP — what separates them is OIS, sensor quality, and video capability*

All three phones have 50MP main cameras. The number alone means nothing — what matters is what is behind it.

The **Infinix Note 60 Pro** brings a 50MP shooter with **OIS (Optical Image Stabilization)** and 4K video recording at 30fps. OIS is the real differentiator. Night shots have less blur, handheld videos are smoother, and overall image consistency is better across different lighting conditions. If you create content for YouTube or TikTok, you will feel the difference from the first recording session.

The **Infinix Hot 60 Pro** has a 50MP main camera without OIS, using software-based EIS for video stabilization instead. Daylight photography is capable and social media-ready. Where you will notice the gap is in low-light photography and video — EIS is not as smooth or reliable as optical stabilization. Still a solid camera for everyday use and social media content.

The **Tecno Spark 40 Pro** packs a 50MP camera with Tecno's AI RAW domain algorithm, which helps recover detail in backlit and night scenes. FlashSnap enables fast capture for moving subjects. Results are good for the price and Tecno's camera processing has improved noticeably from earlier Spark models. It is not the most impressive camera in this comparison but it will not embarrass you in everyday use.

**Camera winner: Infinix Note 60 Pro**

## Verdict — Which One Should You Buy?

**Buy the Infinix Note 60 Pro if** you want the strongest all-round package in this comparison. Snapdragon performance, 6,500mAh battery, 90W charging, OIS camera, Android 16, and a top-tier AMOLED display. Yes, it costs more — but the gap in chipset performance, battery capacity, and charging speed is real and felt in daily use. If you can find it at official Nigerian retail pricing, it is the obvious choice. Also the best long-term investment if you plan to use this phone for four or more years. **Buy the Infinix Hot 60 Pro if** you cannot stretch to Note 60 Pro pricing or you want a phone with proven wider availability right now. The display is excellent, the Helio G200 handles daily life competently, and the battery is sufficient. In isolation it is a genuinely good phone and a reasonable choice for someone who wants a smooth display experience at a lower price point.

**Buy the Tecno Spark 40 Pro if** you are primarily an everyday user — calls, WhatsApp, social media, casual gaming — and you want Tecno's reliable software experience with strong local after-sales support. It is the most budget-friendly of the three, looks premium for the price, and Tecno service centres are accessible across Nigeria. For buyers who want maximum value without maximum price, this is practical sense.

My pick with my own money today? **Infinix Note 60 Pro** — once it is available at normal Nigerian retail pricing from official channels. Everything else is a step down from what it offers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the Infinix Note 60 Pro available in Nigeria right now?**
The Note 60 Pro launched globally in March 2026 and is rolling out gradually in Nigeria. Check Jumia and Slot outlets for current stock. Expect pricing to settle around ₦260,000–₦350,000 as availability improves.

**Which is better for gaming — Infinix Hot 60 Pro or Tecno Spark 40 Pro?**
The Hot 60 Pro wins. The Helio G200 is a stronger gaming chip than the Helio G100 Ultimate, and the higher resolution AMOLED display makes a visual difference. The Spark 40 Pro manages PUBG at 60fps though, so casual gamers will not be left behind.

**Does the Infinix Note 60 Pro support 5G in Nigeria?**
The 5G variant does, but the 4G variant does not. Since 5G coverage in Nigeria is still very limited in 2026, the 4G variant is the more practical choice for most buyers and tends to be more readily available.

**Which phone is best for content creation and YouTube?**
The Infinix Note 60 Pro — OIS and 4K video at 30fps make a real difference in footage quality. If you create content regularly, the stabilized video alone justifies the price difference.

**What is the current price of the Infinix Hot 60 Pro in Nigeria?**
Starting from around ₦223,600 for the 8GB/128GB variant as of early 2026. Prices vary by store so always confirm with the seller before committing.

**Is the Tecno Spark 40 Pro still worth buying in 2026?**
Yes, for everyday use. It handles calls, social media, and casual gaming well, the design looks premium, and Tecno's service network in Nigeria is strong. Not for power users, but honest value for the everyday Nigerian buyer.
