## Overview 

If you've been comparing TECNO phones on Jumia or walking through Computer Village trying to figure out what actually fits under $150, you already know the problem: the naira price on the sticker rarely matches the dollar figure by the time you convert it. Using an exchange rate of roughly ₦1,400 to $1, $150 works out to about ₦205,000–₦215,000 — but that rate moves week to week, so treat it as a reference point and check the live rate before you commit to a purchase.  
  
The Pop and Spark series are where TECNO competes most aggressively on price, battery life, and display features, which is why every phone below comes from one of those two lines. Prices were checked across current Nigerian retail listings (Jumia's official TECNO store, Slot, Pointek, and NaijaTechGuide pricing pages) in August 2026 and rounded to the nearest ₦1,000. Not every phone here is an equally strong buy — the list is ordered from strongest overall pick to the ones that only make sense for a specific budget or use case, and each section says plainly where a phone stands.  
  
**Our ranking criteria:** We ranked these phones on current Nigerian price, chipset performance, battery life and charging speed, display quality, storage options, and how easy each model is to actually find at Nigerian retailers in 2026. Newer isn't automatically ranked higher — the Pop 8 and Spark 10 are both older devices that only make this list because their low price gives them a clear, specific use case, not because they compete head-to-head with the Pop 20 or Spark 30C.

## 1. TECNO Pop 20 — Best Overall Pick  
  
The Pop 20 is one of TECNO's newest entry-level options for 2026, and it's the one worth stretching your budget for if you can. It runs on the Unisoc T7250 chipset with 4GB of RAM (expandable to 8GB via virtual RAM), and the 6.75-inch display runs at 120Hz, which is unusually smooth for this price bracket. The 5,000mAh battery with 15W wired charging comfortably covers a full day of WhatsApp, streaming, and social media, and the phone carries an IP64 rating plus 1.2-metre drop resistance — genuinely useful if you're rough on phones or caught in the rain often.  
  
It also ships with FreeLink 2.0, which lets it send Bluetooth voice calls and texts to another FreeLink-enabled TECNO device within roughly 1.5km, without either phone needing a network signal. The limitation is that it only works between two FreeLink-capable TECNO devices — it's not a way to call or message someone outside that setup, so treat it as a niche backup feature for two people who both own compatible phones, not a general offline-communication tool. We covered the Pop 20 in more depth in our [Pop 20 review](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/tecno-pop-20-review-best-budget-phone-2026/).

## 2. TECNO Spark 30C — Best for Gaming and Multitasking  
  
The Spark 30C we're recommending here is the 4G model, which runs on a single MediaTek Helio G81 chipset — not to be confused with the separate Spark 30C 5G, which uses different hardware entirely (see the FAQ below). The Helio G81 handles Free Fire, PUBG Mobile Lite, and everyday multitasking better than most phones in this bracket, and the dual stereo speakers make it a decent media phone too. It also carries an IP54 rating for dust and splash resistance.  
  
It comes in four Nigerian-market configurations — 4GB/128GB, 6GB/128GB, 4GB/256GB, and 8GB/256GB — and the RAM/storage jump is what moves the price around. The 6GB and 8GB variants push closer to the $150 ceiling, so the 4GB/128GB version is the sweet spot if you're watching your budget. Before you buy, confirm with the seller that the listing says "Spark 30C" and not "Spark 30C 5G" — the names look nearly identical on some marketplace listings.

**Best for:** casual mobile gamers who don't want to spend Camon-series money.  
  
## 3. TECNO Pop 9 4G — Best Value for Everyday Use  
  
Quick clarification first: TECNO sells two phones under the "Pop 9" name — a Pop 9 5G variant with a MediaTek Dimensity 6300 chipset and a 48MP camera, and the Pop 9 4G, which is the one that actually launched in African markets and is what you'll find listed in Nigeria. The two get confused constantly across retailer sites, so make sure any "Pop 9" listing you're looking at specifies 4G before comparing prices.  
  
The Pop 9 4G runs on a Unisoc T615 chipset with 3GB or 4GB of RAM and 64GB or 128GB of storage, paired with a 6.67-inch IPS display running at 120Hz. The rear camera is a single 13MP sensor with autofocus, the front camera is 8MP, and the 5,000mAh battery charges at 15W. It also carries an IP54 splash-resistance rating. It sits in the middle of the lineup on price, sometimes dipping close to ₦114,000 on promo, and while it won't handle demanding 3D games, it's dependable for calls, chat apps, and everyday browsing.

## 4. TECNO Pop 8 — Cheapest Way In (Budget Pick, Not a "Best Overall")  
  
If your budget is closer to $70 than $150, the Pop 8 is where TECNO's line effectively starts. It's an older device — it launched in late 2023 — running a Unisoc T606 chipset with 2GB to 4GB of RAM, a 6.6-inch 90Hz display, a single 13MP rear camera, an 8MP front camera, and a 5,000mAh battery limited to 10W charging. None of that will win benchmarks in 2026, but it covers calls, chat apps, and light browsing without complaint. Think of this one as the cheapest functional entry point into the brand, not a competitive pick against the Pop 20 or Spark 30C.  
  
![TECNO Pop 8 smartphone in Nigeria](/assets/posts/tecno-pop-8-placeholder.jpg)

## 5. TECNO Spark 10 — Ultra-Budget / Backup Pick (Not a Top Contender)  
  
Full disclosure on this one: the Spark 10 launched back in March 2023, which makes it nearly three years old by the time you're reading this. It's included here because it's genuinely the cheapest usable Android phone still sold new in Nigeria under the TECNO name, at ₦75,000–₦90,000 — not because it competes with the newer phones on this list. It runs a MediaTek Helio G37 chipset, a 6.6-inch 90Hz display, 4GB or 8GB of RAM, and a 5,000mAh battery on 10W charging.  
  
If you want one of the five best TECNO phones you can buy right now, this isn't it — the Pop 20 or Spark 30C are the better use of your money if you can stretch the budget. But as a spare device to keep a second SIM active, run a WhatsApp Business line, or hand down to a family member, the Spark 10's rock-bottom price still makes it worth a mention.

## A Quick Note on Nigeria-Specific Buying  
  
Stick to authorized retailers (Jumia's official TECNO store, Slot, Pointek) rather than unverified Jiji listings if you want a working warranty. TECNO's official African/Nigerian-market units are built for the LTE bands local networks use, but grey-market imports sourced from other regions occasionally ship with different band support — ask the seller to confirm the model is the official Nigerian variant, and test the SIM in-store on your own network before paying if you can. And because naira pricing shifts with the exchange rate, a phone that's $140 today can slide past $150 in a few weeks; check the current listing price rather than relying on last month's figure.

## FAQ  
  
**Is TECNO a good brand for budget phones in Nigeria?**  
Yes. TECNO is a Transsion-owned brand built specifically for African markets, with wide retail availability, local warranty support, and software (HiOS) tuned for lower-RAM devices. It's one of the most common budget brands sold through Jumia, Slot, and Pointek.  
  
**Which TECNO phone under $150 has the best battery life?**  
All five phones here use a 5,000mAh battery, which is standard for this segment. The Spark 30C charges fastest at 18W, followed by the Pop 20 and Pop 9 4G at 15W. The Pop 8 and Spark 10 are both capped at 10W, so they'll take noticeably longer to charge.  
  
**Do TECNO phones under $150 support 5G in Nigeria?**  
No — every model recommended in this guide is a 4G device. This gets confusing because TECNO reuses some model names across 4G and 5G versions: there's a separate Spark 30C 5G and a separate Pop 9 5G, each with different chipsets from the 4G phones covered here. If a listing doesn't clearly specify whether it's the 4G or 5G version, ask the seller to confirm the exact model and chipset before paying — 5G variants are generally priced higher and outside this budget anyway.  
  
**Is it better to buy new or fairly-used (UK-used) at this price point?**  
A fairly-used mid-range TECNO or Infinix phone can sometimes outperform a brand-new entry-level one for similar money — see our [Infinix under $300 comparison](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-infinix-phones-under-300/) for that trade-off. If you go fairly-used, buy from a vendor who lets you test the battery health and camera before paying.  
  
**Where can I buy these phones in Nigeria?**  
Jumia, Slot, Pointek, and Konga are the most reliable for new units with warranty. Jiji and Facebook Marketplace often have lower prices but carry more risk — inspect the phone in person before paying.
