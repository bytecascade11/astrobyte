## Overview 

If you've walked into Computer Village or scrolled Slot's website lately, you'll notice Samsung doesn't make it simple anymore. There's the S26 Ultra flexing a 200MP camera, the new Z Flip 8 folding in half like it's showing off, and somewhere in the middle a whole shelf of A-series phones that all look the same from three feet away.

This guide was first published in July and rebuilt in late September 2026 against fresh Nigerian listings, because a lot moved in between. The Z Flip 8 went on sale, the A57 and A37 are now Samsung's current mid-rangers (with the A56 and A36 as last generation's models), and S26 Ultra prices landed well below the ₦1.9 million-plus figures quoted here in July.

One thing worth saying upfront: Naira prices for Samsung phones swing hard depending on where you buy. In mid-September, typical asking prices for a brand-new S26 Ultra on Jiji were around ₦1.375 million for 256GB and ₦1.6 million for 512GB, with individual offers running from roughly ₦1.35 million to about ₦1.88 million. Authorized retailers with warranty backing can price differently again. Treat every figure below as a starting point for negotiation, not gospel. Always confirm the exact model number (the SM-xxxx code) and IMEI before you pay anyone — more on that in the buying section below.

## The flagship tier: S26 Ultra, S26+, and S26

The Galaxy S26 Ultra is still the phone Samsung wants you to aspire to, and it mostly earns it. It runs a 6.9-inch LTPO AMOLED panel, a quad rear camera anchored by a 200MP main sensor with a 5x periscope zoom, and comes in 256GB/12GB, 512GB/12GB, and 1TB/16GB configurations. The chip is technically the "Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy" — an overclocked variant Samsung gets exclusivity on outside China, clocked slightly higher than the standard Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 found in other 2026 flagships. Charging jumped to 60W wired (up from 45W) via Samsung's new Super Fast Charging 3.0, and Samsung's official claim is 75% in 30 minutes — GSMArena's own lab testing actually measured slightly better than that in practice, so the number isn't just marketing padding.

**Nigeria price (September 2026):** typical asking price around ₦1.375 million for 256GB and ₦1.6 million for 512GB on Jiji listings, with 256GB offers spread between about ₦1.35 million and ₦1.88 million. That spread is the point: the cheap end of the range is where fakes, locked units, and wrong-region models live, so run the checks in the buying section.

**Pros:** one of Samsung's strongest zoom-camera systems, seven years of promised OS updates, genuinely fast charging, Privacy Display for public use.
**Cons:** battery capacity (5000mAh) hasn't grown despite the bigger chip, still the heaviest and priciest phone in the lineup, base 256GB will feel tight if you shoot a lot of 8K video.

If that price makes you wince, the standard S26 is worth a serious look. It packs a 4,300mAh battery (up from 4,000mAh on the S25) into a 6.3-inch one-handed body, running the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 in most regions and the Exynos 2600 in Europe. Charging stays at 25W wired — Samsung didn't extend the S26 Ultra's charging upgrade down the line, so don't expect Ultra-speed top-ups here. Storage starts at 256GB with 12GB RAM as standard now; the old 128GB base tier is gone. In Nigeria, the typical asking price for a new 256GB S26 was around ₦1.1 million in mid-September.

**Pros:** genuinely compact flagship, bigger battery than last year, same core Snapdragon silicon as the Ultra in most markets.
**Cons:** no periscope zoom, slower 25W charging, no Privacy Display feature.

If screen size is your priority over zoom, the S26+ sits in between with a 6.7-inch display and a 4,900mAh battery, though it's harder to find in Nigerian retail listings than the base and Ultra models — we couldn't find enough listings to price it reliably. For a deeper look at how these three actually hold up on a full day's use, our <a href="https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/samsung-battery-life/">Samsung battery life breakdown</a> is worth reading before you decide between them, and if camera output specifically is what's driving your decision, we've ranked the S-series against Samsung's other camera-forward models in our <a href="https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/top-samsung-phones-best-camera/">best Samsung camera phones guide</a>. ## The A-series: where most Nigerians actually buy

This is the tier that matters for volume, and Samsung clearly knows it. One change since July: Samsung's current mid-range pair is the Galaxy A57 and A37, with the A56 and A36 now the previous generation. The older pair is still widely sold and can still be the smart buy — but only if the price is actually lower.

**The new generation.** The A57 (8GB/256GB) is listed at ₦698,750 at Slot, and retailers earlier this year listed the 8GB/128GB version at around ₦623,500. It keeps the 6.7-inch 120Hz Super AMOLED display and 5000mAh battery, and steps up to IP68 dust and water resistance in a slimmer 6.9mm body. The A37 sits below it; we couldn't find a reliable September price for it, so check current listings from a couple of sellers before budgeting around it.

**The previous generation.** The Galaxy A56 and Galaxy A36 sit close enough on paper that people genuinely confuse them at the point of sale, so here's the actual difference: the A56 runs Samsung's own Exynos 1580 chip, while the A36 uses a Qualcomm Snapdragon 6 Gen 3. Both ship with a 6.7-inch 120Hz Super AMOLED display rated at 1900 nits peak brightness, a 5000mAh battery, 45W charging, and IP67 water resistance — which, if you've ever caught a downpour on your way from a bus stop in Benin or Lagos, is not a small thing.

The A56 is typically sold in 8GB RAM configurations with 128GB or 256GB storage, though 12GB RAM variants show up in some Nigerian listings, likely grey-market imports from other regions. The A36 offers more spread — 6GB, 8GB, or 12GB RAM paired with 128GB or 256GB storage — so check the exact configuration before comparing prices between two "A36" listings.

On price, the A36 (6GB/128GB) sits around ₦580,000–₦660,000 at the sources we checked: GSMArena Nigeria showed ₦581,000 in August, and Ziggatech listed ₦662,800 on 27 September. The A56 (8GB/256GB) was ₦827,800 at Ziggatech that same day — and that's the trap. One retailer's A56 now costs more than Slot's A57 with the same memory, so always ask for the A57 price before you commit to the older model. Compare the same seller, the same RAM, and the same storage.

**A57 pros:** IP68 rating, slimmer body, current-generation hardware.
**A57 cons:** higher entry price than the A36 and A37, and less local price history to negotiate against.

**A56 pros:** 6-year OS and security update promise, sharp 1900-nit display for outdoor visibility, reliable Samsung camera processing.
**A56 cons:** Exynos chips have historically run warmer under sustained load than Qualcomm equivalents; camera front sensor specs vary between listings, so verify before buying.

**A36 pros:** same 120Hz AMOLED and IP67 rating for less money in most listings, Snapdragon silicon some buyers simply prefer.
**A36 cons:** more RAM/storage combinations to sort through when shopping, so it's easier to accidentally overpay for a lower-spec unit.

On the gaming question specifically — I'd stop short of calling either chip the clear winner. Real-world gaming performance depends heavily on how well each app is optimized for the chip and how the phone manages heat during long sessions, not just raw benchmark numbers. Some reviewers find the Snapdragon 6 Gen 3 holds clocks slightly better under sustained load, but thermal throttling behavior varies by unit, ambient temperature, and even which case you're using. Neither is a dedicated gaming chip either way — for that, our <a href="https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-phones-for-gaming-under-250/">gaming phones under $250 guide</a> covers dedicated options that outperform both at similar money.

## The foldable question: Z Flip 8 (and the Z Flip 7 alternative)

The Z Flip is the phone people buy to be seen with it, and there's nothing wrong with that as a reason. Samsung announced the Z Flip 8 on July 22 and it has been on sale since August. It keeps the 6.9-inch inner AMOLED panel and 4.1-inch cover FlexWindow display of last year's model, but gets thinner and lighter (6.1mm unfolded, 180g) and moves to the 2nm Exynos 2600 on the international model sold outside the US, Canada, and China. RAM is 12GB, with 256GB or 512GB storage and no microSD slot. The battery is still 4,300mAh with 25W wired charging, and the IP48 rating is unchanged — so the dust-protection caveat below still applies.

**Nigerian pricing:** we couldn't find a reliable Nigerian retail price for the Flip 8 this month — listings are still thin and inconsistent, and the naira figures that circulate are mostly currency conversions of South African or US prices, which aren't what you'll pay at a Lagos counter. Get at least two current quotes from authorized sellers before paying.

**The Z Flip 7 alternative.** The previous model — Exynos 2500 chip, 12GB RAM, 256GB or 512GB storage, and the same 4,300mAh battery — is still everywhere in Nigerian listings. Its pricing is all over the place: one September price index put the 512GB model around ₦850,000 new, while older retailer pages still show ₦1.5 million and up. That gap is exactly why a fresh quote matters more than any number in a guide. Compared with the Flip 8, you give up the thinner body and newer chip; if the price gap is real, that can be a fair trade.

**Pros:** noticeably larger, more usable cover screen; battery efficiency gains on the Flip 8; satellite connectivity support on recent Flips.
**Cons:** 25W charging feels slow at this price point; IP48 is well short of the S-series' IP68; hinge mechanisms remain a long-term durability consideration on any foldable.

## Who should buy what

If you're doing content creation, running a business off your phone, or just refuse to charge twice a day, the S26 Ultra's zoom camera and faster charging justify the price for a certain kind of buyer. If you want flagship speed without flagship bulk, the standard S26 is the pragmatic pick, though its 25W charging is worth knowing about upfront.

For most budgets under about ₦700,000, the A-series is still where Samsung's value sits, but the price equation has changed. Start with the A57 and A37, then check whether a discounted A36 or A56 actually beats them at the same seller — prices this September ran ₦580,000 to ₦660,000 for the A36, around ₦700,000 for an A57 (8GB/256GB), and higher for the A56 at some retailers. Either generation should comfortably handle a two-year ownership period for typical users who aren't gaming heavily. If you're spending well under that figure, Samsung is not automatically the best value in Nigeria, and the broader roundups linked below are worth a look before you commit.

## Where to buy genuine Samsung phones in Nigeria

The safest route is Samsung's own Experience Stores or listed authorized partners, since these carry Samsung Nigeria warranty support that grey-market imports don't. Online, Jumia and Konga sell Samsung stock with return windows, which gives you some recourse if a unit arrives faulty. Slot and other established retail chains with physical locations in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, and other major cities are generally reliable for new units, though prices tend to run slightly higher than Computer Village or Jiji listings in exchange for warranty backing.

If you're buying from Computer Village, Jiji, or any individual seller, do these checks before handing over money:

- Dial `*#06#` on the phone to display the IMEI, then verify it matches the box and isn't flagged as stolen or blacklisted.
- Confirm the model number (e.g., SM-A566B for the A56) matches what's advertised — regional variants sometimes ship with different chipsets or missing bands. The suffix is only a hint: B generally marks the international model, while U, U1, and W usually mark US or Canadian variants that can be carrier-locked. It doesn't make a phone a safe buy on its own — region, supported bands, lock status, warranty, and the exact model all matter.
- Ask specifically whether the warranty is Samsung Nigeria-issued or an international/UK warranty, since only the former gets you local service center support.
- Inspect the IP67/IP68 seal areas (SIM tray gasket) on any unit claiming water resistance, since a compromised seal voids that protection entirely.
- Make the seller sign out of their Google account in front of you — a phone still tied to someone else's account can lock itself after a factory reset.
- Never pay in full before powering on the device and confirming it isn't a refurbished or duplicate-IMEI unit.

## FAQ

**Which is better value in Nigeria: the Galaxy A57, A56, or A36?**
Compare the same seller, the same RAM, and the same storage. The A36 has been the cheapest of the three at most sellers we checked (roughly ₦580,000–₦660,000 for 6GB/128GB), while the A57 (8GB/256GB) was listed at ₦698,750 at Slot — and at one retailer the older A56 with the same memory costs more than that. Gaming performance varies more by thermal management and app optimization than by chip alone, so don't treat any of them as a decisive winner on that front. If prices converge in your local market, go with the newer phone or whichever seller offers better warranty support.

**Should I buy the S26 Ultra or the S25 Ultra?**
The S25 Ultra is still a capable flagship, but be careful with price comparisons: the only current figures we have come from different sellers, so they aren't a controlled comparison. Ziggatech, a retailer, listed the 512GB S25 Ultra at ₦1,356,800 on 27 September, while the S26 Ultra 512GB figure (a typical ₦1.6 million) is a Jiji asking-price average. That suggests a gap of roughly ₦245,000, but treat it as a rough signal, not a like-for-like difference. At 256GB the gap looks much thinner. The S26 Ultra's main upgrades are faster 60W charging, camera refinements, and the Privacy Display feature — worth it if you use banking or work apps in public, less critical otherwise.

**Do Samsung phones bought in Nigeria come with local warranty?**
Only if purchased from Samsung's official Experience Stores or authorized partners. Grey-market imports common at Computer Village and on Jiji often carry international or seller-only warranties, meaning no Samsung Nigeria service center support if something breaks. Always ask before paying.

**How many years of software updates do these phones get?**
The S26 series and the Z Flip 7 and 8 are promised seven years of major Android updates. The A56 and A36 get six years of OS and security updates — genuinely strong for the mid-range tier and ahead of most competitors at the same price. For the full picture across the lineup, see our <a href="https://www.revibyte.blog/samsung/how-long-do-samsung-phones-get-updates/">Samsung software support guide</a>.

**Is the Z Flip 8 (or Flip 7) durable enough for daily use in Nigeria's climate and roads?**
Samsung's recent foldables have held up well over the past few generations after early durability concerns with the original Fold line. That said, foldables remain more delicate than slab phones by design — a hinge is still a moving mechanical part, and the IP48 rating on both the Flip 7 and Flip 8 is lower than the IP68 rating on the S26 series, meaning less dust protection. A case and mindful pocket habits go a long way.
