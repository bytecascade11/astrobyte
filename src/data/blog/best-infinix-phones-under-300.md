## Overview 

₦300,000 used to get you much closer to flagship territory in Nigeria. Now it's a sweet spot where Infinix packs AMOLED screens, fast charging, 5G, and increasingly capable chipsets into phones that cost far less than premium models. The catch is that Infinix now has so many overlapping series — Hot, Note, Smart, Zero — that picking the right one without wasting money on the wrong config feels harder than it should be.  
  
**Price checked:** August 11, 2026 · **Sources:** Jumia NG, Slot, 3C Hub listings, and NaijaTechGuide's price coverage · **Typical configuration referenced:** 8GB/128GB unless noted otherwise. Prices swing weekly with the dollar rate and vary by retailer, so treat the ranges below as a shopping guide, not a fixed price list — always compare at least two sources before paying.  
  
One more thing worth flagging upfront: Infinix sells multiple variants of some of these phones with different chipsets depending on the region and model number (for example, the standard Note 60 runs MediaTek's Dimensity 7400 Ultimate, while the separate Note 60 Pro switches to a Snapdragon 7s Gen 4). Where that applies, I've called it out below — but always confirm the exact model number on the listing before you buy, since sellers don't always separate them clearly.  
  
## How I ranked these  
  
I weighed five things when narrowing this list down: price relative to ₦300k, performance for everyday and light gaming use, display quality, battery/charging, and camera — roughly in that order, since most buyers in this price bracket care more about not lagging and not running out of battery than about camera specs.

## 1. Infinix Note 60 — the one I'd actually buy  
  
![Infinix Note 60 in Mist Titanium color](/assets/posts/infinix-note-60.jpg)  
  
The Note 60 is doing something Infinix rarely pulls off this cleanly: giving you a phone that doesn't feel like a compromise. You get a proper AMOLED panel instead of the usual budget-tier IPS, a Dimensity 7400 Ultimate that handles everyday multitasking and light gaming without hiccups, and a 6,500mAh battery — Infinix's largest to date on this series — that should hold up well for a full day of heavy WhatsApp, social media, and streaming use, though actual endurance will still depend on your brightness settings, network signal, and how much gaming you do. Storage starts at 8GB/128GB, with an 8GB/256GB option for anyone who shoots a lot of video. Just note this is the standard Note 60, not the pricier Note 60 Pro, which swaps in a Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 chipset — check the model number before buying if you want to be sure which one you're getting. If you can only pick one phone from this list, this is my recommendation — it just does the least amount of annoying you.  
  
## 2. Infinix Hot 60 Pro+ — closest thing to flagship money can buy here  
  
![Infinix Hot 60 Pro plus rear camera design](/assets/posts/infinix-hot-60-pro-plus.jpg)  
  
This is the "Hot" series stretching itself right up to the ₦300k ceiling, and mostly justifying it. The AMOLED display here — a 144Hz panel Infinix upscales from a 1080p render — is genuinely nice for streaming Nollywood or football highlights, and the slim, light build feels noticeably more premium in hand than the Hot name usually suggests. The Helio G200 is a mid-tier chipset built for everyday tasks rather than heavy gaming, so treat it as capable for browsing, calls, and casual games rather than a performance pick. If you care more about screen quality and design than chasing benchmark numbers, this edges out the Note 60 for some buyers — just note it's a 4G phone, unlike the Note 60.

## 4. Infinix Note 50 Pro — still worth it if the price drops right  
  
![Infinix Note 50 Pro camera setup](/assets/posts/infinix-note-50-pro.jpg)  
  
A year older than the Note 60, but the Note 50 Pro hasn't aged out of relevance — especially now that prices have softened. It still packs a 144Hz AMOLED screen, the same capable Helio G100 Ultimate chipset, and 90W charging that gets you from empty to full faster than most phones twice its price. If you find it comfortably under ₦260,000, it's arguably better value than paying full price for the newer Note 60.  
  
## 5. Infinix Hot 60i — for the tightest budget on this list  
  
![Infinix Hot 60i affordable smartphone](/assets/posts/infinix-hot-60i.jpg)  
  
Not everyone wants to spend close to ₦300k, and the Hot 60i exists for exactly that reason. It's an IPS screen instead of AMOLED and a more modest Helio G81 Ultimate chip, but it still runs a smooth 120Hz refresh rate and covers calls, social media, and light gaming without drama. Current listings can range from around ₦170,000 to ₦265,000 for the 8GB configuration, so shop around before paying full price.  
  
## How I'd choose between these  
  
If you want zero regrets and can stretch the budget, get the **Note 60**. If display quality matters more than raw specs, take the **Hot 60 Pro+**. Gamers should lean toward the **Hot 60 Pro**, camera-and-charging fans should check the **Note 50 Pro** for a discount, and if money is genuinely tight, the **Hot 60i** still gets the basics right.  
  
## FAQ  
  
**Are these prices fixed across Nigeria?**  
No. Infinix doesn't enforce a single retail price, so the same phone can differ by ₦30,000–₦50,000 between Jumia, Slot, 3C Hub, and Jiji depending on stock, promos, and the dollar rate that week. Always compare at least two sources before paying.  
  
**Which of these five actually supports 5G?**  
The Note 60 supports 5G, and the Note 50 Pro has both 4G and 5G variants depending on the specific SKU. The Hot 60 Pro, Hot 60 Pro+, and Hot 60i are 4G-only. 5G availability depends on the exact model number and regional variant, so check the Nigerian retailer's specification sheet before buying rather than assuming from the series name alone — this is fine either way if your area doesn't have reliable 5G coverage yet.  
  
**Is buying from Jiji or a phone market riskier than Jumia?**  
It can be, mainly around warranty and authenticity. If you go that route, ask for the original carton, check the IMEI against the box, and test the phone thoroughly before paying — especially for "used" or "UK-used" listings.  
  
**Should I wait for Black Friday or December sales instead?**  
If your current phone still works, waiting can save real money — Infinix prices in Nigeria typically dip during major sales periods. If you need a phone now, don't gamble on a discount that might not beat what's already on the market.  
  
**Do any of these have expandable storage?**  
Most current Infinix models support virtual RAM expansion, but actual microSD expansion varies by model and region, so check the exact variant's spec sheet on the retailer's listing before buying if this matters to you.
