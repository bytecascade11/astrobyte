## Overview

Realme spent its early years chasing headline specs — the fastest charging, the biggest number on a box. In 2026 the strategy has matured. The lineup now stretches from a genuine Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 flagship down to a rugged sub-₦300,000 workhorse, and each tier actually has a clear reason to exist instead of overlapping with the model above it.

If you're trying to work out which Realme fits your budget and how it holds up in Nigeria — where import routes, band compatibility, and naira pricing swing wildly by retailer — here's the breakdown, model by model. ## Realme GT 8 Pro — the flagship that finally earns the price tag

The GT 8 Pro is Realme's most serious flagship attempt yet. It runs the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 alongside a dedicated R1 graphics chip and a large vapour-chamber cooling system, paired with a 6.79-inch 2K AMOLED panel that hits a 144Hz refresh rate and — depending on the market variant — brightness figures that go well past what most flagships offer. The camera system leans on Ricoh-tuned optics with a 200MP main sensor alongside dual 50MP units, and the phone carries IP66/IP68/IP69 water and dust resistance, which is unusually thorough for a device in this class.

Battery life is handled by a 7000mAh cell with 120W wired charging, and the phone ships with Android 16 and a promise of multiple years of major OS updates. The trade-offs are real, though: there's no LTPO panel, so the display can't drop its refresh rate as aggressively to save power, and the USB port is capped at USB 2.0 speeds — an odd corner to cut on a phone this expensive.

Realme hasn't given the GT 8 Pro an official Nigerian retail launch, so it's currently only available through import and grey-market listings. Expect to pay a significant premium over the international sticker price, and always confirm IMEI registration and warranty terms before buying one this way.

Realme dropped the Pro+ tier from the 15 series entirely, and its curved-screen design flagship duties now sit with the 16 Pro+. It runs the same Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 chipset as the 15 Pro but adds a 200MP main camera with a 50MP periscope telephoto capable of roughly 3.5x optical zoom, a 6.8-inch AMOLED display with up to 6,500-nit peak brightness, and IP68/IP69K water resistance. The 7000mAh battery carries over from the rest of the numbered lineup.

Nigerian pricing is unusually inconsistent for this model — some retailers list it close to ₦600,000, others well past ₦1,000,000 for higher storage tiers or eco-leather finishes. Given that spread, treat any single naira figure you see online as a starting point only, and compare at least two or three listings before buying.

## Realme 15 Pro — most of the design, a meaningfully lower price

The 15 Pro trims the curved-screen flourish but keeps a 6.8-inch OLED panel at 144Hz, a dual 50MP rear camera setup, and a 7000mAh battery with 80W charging — a genuinely large cell for this price bracket. It runs a Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 chipset, which handles everyday use, streaming, and light-to-moderate gaming comfortably without pretending to be a flagship-tier processor.

Nigerian listings vary widely, but a realistic range sits around ₦350,000–₦480,000. As with the 16 Pro+, shop around and confirm current stock before committing to a number.

## Realme 15 — the sensible mid-ranger

![Realme 15 in Suit Titanium colorway](/assets/posts/realme-15-titanium.jpg)

The standard 15 drops down to a Dimensity 7300+ chipset but keeps a 6.77-inch AMOLED display at 144Hz and the same 7000mAh battery with 80W charging as the Pro model. The camera setup is simpler — a 50MP main plus 8MP ultrawide rather than the Pro's arrangement — but for buyers who mainly use their phone for social media, calls, and streaming, that's rarely a dealbreaker.

Nigerian pricing is comparatively stable at around ₦180,000–₦230,000, which makes the 15 one of the better-value entries in the entire lineup if you don't need flagship-adjacent styling.

## Realme C75 — the budget pick built to survive Nigerian daily use

![Realme C75 rugged design with ArmorShell glass](/assets/posts/realme-c75-rugged-design.jpg)

The C75 targets a different priority entirely: durability over spec-sheet bragging rights. It's built with ArmorShell glass and carries a TÜV Rheinland ruggedness certification, IP66/IP68 water resistance, and MIL-STD-810H shock testing — features you don't typically see below ₦300,000. The 6000mAh battery with 45W charging is tuned more for longevity than speed, and the MediaTek Helio G92 Max chipset is adequate for basic apps and light gaming, not demanding titles.

A 5G variant exists as well, built on a Dimensity 6300 chipset instead, for buyers who want future network compatibility without paying Pro-series prices. Nigerian pricing for the 4G C75 generally falls between ₦230,000 and ₦290,000, with the 5G version priced a little higher depending on the retailer. If your budget sits lower still, our [best itel phones guide](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-itel-phone-2026/) covers the tier just below this one.

## Which one should you actually buy?

If you want the fastest chipset and camera hardware Realme makes and don't mind sourcing it through an import channel, the **GT 8 Pro** is the clear pick. For most Nigerian buyers who want strong day-to-day performance and a big battery without flagship pricing, the **15 Pro** is the stronger all-rounder than the 16 Pro+, since the curved-screen design premium and periscope zoom don't always translate into a meaningfully better daily experience for the extra cost. And if durability and battery life under ₦300,000 matter more than raw speed, the **C75** remains one of the few phones in that price band with genuine ruggedness certification.

## FAQ

**Does Realme officially sell phones in Nigeria?**
Yes — Realme has an official Nigeria storefront and sells models like the C-series and numbered series through authorized retailers. Flagship GT-series phones, however, are typically only available through import listings rather than an official local launch.

**Are Realme phones good value compared to [Tecno](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-tecno-phone-2026/), Infinix, or Redmi in the same price range?**
Generally yes on raw specs — Realme tends to include larger batteries and higher refresh-rate displays at a given price point — but software update support and after-sales service coverage in Nigeria can be less consistent than more established local brands, so factor that in.

**Which Realme phone has the best battery life?**
The GT 8 Pro, 15 Pro, and 15 all share a 7000mAh cell, but the 15 Pro's more efficient chipset generally gives it the best real-world endurance of the three for typical daily use.

**Is it safe to import the Realme GT 8 Pro into Nigeria?**
It can be, but you lose the manufacturer warranty coverage that comes with official channels. Verify the IMEI, confirm the seller's return policy in writing, and check that the unit supports the 5G and 4G bands used by Nigerian carriers before paying.

**Does the Realme C75 support 5G?**
The standard C75 is 4G-only. A separate C75 5G variant exists with a different chipset (Dimensity 6300) for buyers who specifically need 5G connectivity.
