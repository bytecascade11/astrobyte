## Overview 

TECNO built the Camon line around one promise: better photos than the price tag suggests. In 2026 that promise now sits mainly on the **Camon 50 series** — the Camon 50, Camon 50 Pro, and the newly launched Camon 50 Ultra 5G — which now represents TECNO's newest camera-focused lineup in Nigeria. The Camon 40 series hasn't disappeared from shelves, though, and in a few cases it's still the smarter buy on price.  
  
If you're choosing a Camon purely for the camera — portraits at owambe parties, product shots for a side hustle, or sharper night shots than your last phone gave you — here are the five worth your money right now, ranked by what the sensor and lens combination actually delivers rather than by spec-sheet megapixel counts alone.

TECNO's current camera flagship pairs a 50MP OIS main sensor with a genuine 50MP 3x optical telephoto (also OIS-stabilized) and an 8MP ultrawide, plus a 50MP autofocus selfie camera up front. It runs on the Dimensity 7400 Ultimate chipset with a 6,150mAh battery and 45W charging, and it's rated IP68/IP69K for dust and water resistance.

## 2. TECNO Camon 50 Pro — best camera-to-price balance right now  
  
![TECNO Camon 50 Pro rear camera module](/assets/posts/tecno-camon-50-pro.jpg)  
  
In Nigeria, the Camon 50 Pro ships as a **4G** device on the Helio G200 Ultimate chipset — it's not the same phone as the differently-specced "Pro 5G" sold under the same family name in some other markets, so don't assume the name alone tells you the network or processor. TECNO's official Nigeria spec page lists a 50MP OIS main camera, a genuine 50MP 3x telephoto, an 8MP ultrawide, a 32MP front camera, a 6,150mAh battery, and 45W charging. Nigerian retailers currently list it from around ₦450,000 to ₦540,000.  
  
The Camon 50 Pro shares the same core rear-camera configuration as the Ultra — main sensor, telephoto, and ultrawide are effectively matched on paper. What you're mainly losing versus the Ultra is 5G connectivity, a faster chipset, and the higher-resolution 50MP selfie camera in favour of a 32MP one.  
  
**Best for:** buyers who want the Ultra's rear-camera setup for considerably less money and don't need 5G.  
  
## 3. TECNO Camon 40 Premier 5G — still capable, now worth comparing on price  
  
![TECNO Camon 40 Premier 5G triple-camera module](/assets/posts/tecno-camon-40-premier-5g.jpg)  
  
This was last year's flagship, and the triple 50MP setup — a 50MP OIS main, a genuine 3x telephoto, and a 50MP ultrawide that outresolves the Camon 50 series' 8MP ultrawide — is still a strong combination on paper. The 50MP autofocus selfie camera remains a good pick for content creators. It runs on the Dimensity 8350 Ultimate chipset with a 5,100mAh battery and 70W charging — the fastest charging speed on this list. Current Nigerian listings put it at roughly ₦550,000 to ₦690,000.  
  
The catch: with the Camon 50 Pro now offering a very similar main-camera and telephoto setup at a lower price, the Camon 40 Premier 5G's value case has narrowed. Its clearest remaining advantage is that sharper 50MP ultrawide lens versus the newer series' 8MP unit — worth it if wide-angle shots matter to you specifically, otherwise cross-shop the price against the Camon 50 Pro before deciding.  
  
**Best for:** buyers who specifically want a stronger ultrawide lens and can find it discounted against current Camon 50 pricing.

Both variants share the same rear camera spec on TECNO's own sheet: a 50MP main sensor with OIS paired with an 8MP ultrawide and a 50MP autofocus selfie camera. Where they differ is the chipset — the **5G model uses the Dimensity 7300 Ultimate**, while the **4G model uses the Helio G100 Ultimate**, a meaningfully different performance tier, not just a missing modem. Both get a 5,200mAh battery and 45W charging. The 4G variant is the more consistently available one in Nigerian retail, priced around ₦364,000; the 5G variant tends to sit higher, in the ₦400,000–₦460,000 range depending on stock.  
  
The 4G Pro is the better cheaper alternative rather than a straight discount on an identical phone: you're trading real gaming and multitasking headroom for a lower price, alongside losing 5G. The core camera hardware and processing pipeline are the same specification on both, so photo quality should track closely, though the faster chipset on the 5G model can still affect burst shooting speed and AI processing.  
  
The Camon 40 Pro 5G is also the phone that earned TECNO's first DXOMARK Smart Choice label — a recognition [DXOMARK](https://www.dxomark.com/tecno-camon-40-pro-5g-camera-test/) gives to devices with standout camera performance for their price bracket, and a useful third-party signal if you want more than TECNO's own marketing to go on.  
  
**Best for:** buyers who want DXOMARK-tested camera performance without flagship pricing, and can decide whether 5G is worth the chipset upgrade.  
  
## 5. TECNO Camon 50 (4G) — the budget camera pick  
  
![TECNO Camon 50 4G rear camera module](/assets/posts/tecno-camon-50-4g.jpg)  
  
The standard Camon 50 keeps the same core rear-camera hardware as the Pro — a 50MP OIS main lens plus an 8MP autofocus ultrawide — but drops the telephoto lens and runs on the same Helio G200 Ultimate chipset. Up front it carries a 32MP selfie camera, and the whole package is backed by a 6,150mAh battery with 45W charging. Nigerian retail pricing runs roughly ₦300,000 to ₦380,000.  
  
**Best for:** anyone who wants a competent 50MP main camera and a useful ultrawide without paying for the telephoto lens or 5G.  
  
## Which one should you actually buy?  
  
For most people reading this, the Camon 50 Pro is the sweet spot right now — it carries very similar rear-camera hardware to the flagship Ultra for considerably less money, and its telephoto lens gives it an edge over the Camon 40 Pro on paper. If your budget caps out lower, the Camon 40 Pro 5G still has a genuine DXOMARK-tested reputation behind it and remains a solid buy while its price continues to soften against the newer series. If money is tight, the standard Camon 50 covers the essentials — a competent main camera and a useful ultrawide — without the telephoto premium.  
  
## FAQ  
  
**Does the Camon 50 series support 5G in Nigeria?**  
In Nigeria, only the Camon 50 Ultra 5G is currently sold as a 5G model in the Camon 50 lineup, on supported Nigerian networks and in areas with active 5G coverage. The Camon 50 Pro and standard Camon 50 both ship as 4G devices in Nigeria on the Helio G200 Ultimate chipset — TECNO does sell differently-configured "Pro" variants under 5G branding in some other markets, so the name alone isn't a reliable guide.  
  
**Is the Camon 40 Premier 5G still worth buying now that the Camon 50 series is out?**  
It depends on the price you find it at. Its main camera and telephoto specs are now closely matched by the cheaper Camon 50 Pro, so the Camon 40 Premier 5G's clearest remaining advantage is its sharper 50MP ultrawide lens versus the newer series' 8MP ultrawide. Compare current prices on both before deciding.  
  
**What's the actual difference between the Camon 40 Pro 4G and 5G models?**  
More than just the missing 5G modem. The 5G model uses the Dimensity 7300 Ultimate chipset, while the 4G model uses the Helio G100 Ultimate — a real difference in processing and gaming performance. The core rear camera hardware specification is the same on both.  
  
**Which Camon has the best selfie camera?**  
The Camon 50 Ultra 5G and both Camon 40 Pro variants use a 50MP autofocus front camera. The Camon 50 Pro and standard Camon 50 both use a 32MP front camera without autofocus.  
  
**Do these phones charge fast enough for daily use?**  
The Camon 40 Premier 5G supports 70W wired charging, the fastest on this list, while the Camon 50 series and Camon 40 Pro models top out at 45W. All are fast enough to take a Camon from empty to a usable charge well within an hour, which matters if NEPA has you charging in short windows.
