## Overview 

5G is no longer a "someday" feature in Nigeria — MTN and Airtel have both pushed their networks into more state capitals, and TECNO has responded by folding 5G radios into almost every price bracket it sells, not just the flagships. That's the good news. The less convenient news is that TECNO now ships more 5G-branded phones than most buyers can keep straight, and not all of them justify the naira you'd pay over a solid 4G alternative.  
  
This list narrows it down to five TECNO 5G phones that earn their place in 2026 — one for nearly every kind of buyer, from students who want affordable 5G connectivity to creators who need a camera that can carry a photo shoot.  
  
## How these picks were chosen  
  
Every model here had to clear three bars: it needed a genuine 5G modem (not a 4G phone with a 5G sticker), a chipset that should stay comfortable for everyday multitasking over the next couple of years, and pricing that's been seen across more than one Nigerian retailer rather than a single listing.

## 1. TECNO Spark 30C 5G — best budget 5G phone  
  
If your only goal is "get on 5G without spending real money," this is the phone. It runs a MediaTek Dimensity 6300, pairs it with a 120Hz HD+ display, and still finds room for a 5,000mAh battery and stereo speakers — none of which is standard at this price. The trade-offs are honest ones: an HD+ (not Full HD) screen, plastic build, and 18W charging that feels slow next to its siblings on this list. For students and first-5G-phone buyers, none of that matters much.  
  
> **Naming check:** Make sure you're buying the **Spark 30C 5G**, not the plain **Spark 30C**. TECNO sells both under near-identical names in Nigeria, but the regular Spark 30C is a 4G-only phone built on a MediaTek Helio G81 — no 5G modem at all. TECNO's Nigerian site and most retailers list them as separate products, so double-check the listing title (and ideally the box) says "5G" before you pay.

## 2. TECNO Pova 7 5G — best all-round mid-range pick  
  
The Pova 7 5G takes the same Dimensity 7300 Ultimate chipset that powers TECNO's pricier models and puts it in a phone built around one thing: a battery that lasts. A 6,000mAh cell with 45W wired charging, 30W wireless charging, and 10W reverse charging covers a full day of gaming, streaming, and social media without the anxiety-checking-your-percentage routine — and the wireless charging in particular is a genuinely rare feature to find at this price point. The 144Hz display is a genuine step up for scrolling and light gaming, even if the camera setup is more "good enough" than standout.

## 3. TECNO Pova 7 Pro 5G — best for gaming on a budget  
  
It runs the same MediaTek Dimensity 7300 Ultimate and 6,000mAh battery as the standard Pova 7 5G, but the Pro trades the standard panel for AMOLED and sharpens the whole package for gamers who care about frame consistency. It's the phone to pick if Call of Duty Mobile or eFootball sessions run long and you don't want thermal throttling ruining a match. Pricing overlaps with the standard Camon 40, so shop around before assuming Pro automatically costs more.  
  
> **Spec note:** The 64MP + 8MP ultrawide camera above matches TECNO's official global spec sheet for the Pova 7 Pro 5G, which is the same listing Nigerian retailers pull from. TECNO doesn't publish a separate Nigeria-specific spec page for this model, so it's the most reliable source available — but as with any import-heavy market, it's worth confirming the camera spec printed on the box matches before you buy, especially from a marketplace listing rather than an authorized store.

## 4. TECNO Camon 40 Pro 5G — best camera phone  
  
This is the one worth the extra naira if photography matters to you. The Camon 40 Pro pairs a 50MP main sensor (1/1.56-inch, with OIS) with an 8MP autofocus ultrawide, which is unusual generosity at this price tier — DXOMARK has recognized it as a strong performer for its camera category, and the ultrawide's autofocus is a rare inclusion at this price. It also carries IP68/IP69 water and dust resistance, something none of the other phones on this list offer. The standard (non-Pro) Camon 40 drops the water resistance and OIS but costs noticeably less, so it's worth comparing both in-store.  
  
![TECNO Camon 40 Pro 5G smartphone](/assets/posts/camon-40-pro-5g-placeholder.jpg)  
  
## 5. TECNO Phantom V Flip2 5G — best premium pick  
  
The outlier on this list, and deliberately so. The Phantom V Flip2 is a proper clamshell foldable with a 4,720mAh battery (large for a flip phone), 70W wired charging, and a 3.64-inch cover display that does more than show notifications. To be clear, it's not the most powerful phone TECNO sells in Nigeria — its Dimensity 8020 is a mid-tier chipset, not a flagship one — it's the premium *foldable* pick, chosen for a form factor none of TECNO's slab phones offer, not for raw performance. It's a niche buy in Nigeria — foldables carry real import and repair-cost considerations here — but for buyers who want something genuinely different, it's the closest TECNO gets to a flagship statement.

## Other TECNO 5G phones we considered  
  
TECNO's Nigerian 5G lineup is wider than five phones, and a few names deserve a mention for why they didn't make the cut:  
  
- **POVA Curve 5G** — shares most of its internals with the standard Pova 7 5G but adds a curved display as its main differentiator. Left off because the curve is a design preference rather than a functional upgrade, and Nigerian retail availability has been thinner than the standard Pova 7 5G.  
- **POVA 7 Ultra 5G** — this one genuinely outperforms the Pro. It runs a MediaTek Dimensity 8350 Ultimate — a distinctly higher tier than the Dimensity 7300 Ultimate in the 7 and 7 Pro — paired with a 108MP main camera and 70W wired charging. It's the more performance-oriented phone, full stop, and if it's in stock near you at a comparable price, it's arguably a better gaming buy than the Pova 7 Pro 5G. It's excluded here on availability grounds, not spec grounds: as of August 2026, Nigerian retail trackers list the Pova 7 Ultra 5G as "upcoming" rather than in active stock, meaning most Nigerian buyers can't reliably purchase one yet at a set price. Once it's in regular circulation, it likely displaces the Pova 7 Pro 5G as the gaming pick on this list.  
- **Camon 40 Premier 5G** — sits above the Camon 40 Pro 5G with a stronger camera system, but its Nigerian pricing pushes it closer to the Phantom V Flip2's territory, which makes it a harder sell than the Pro unless camera quality is your only priority.  
- **Standard Camon 40 5G / Spark 30 5G** — close relatives of picks already on this list, dropped mainly for overlap rather than being bad phones; if the Camon 40 Pro or Spark 30C 5G is out of stock, these are reasonable fallbacks.  
  
If any of these fit your budget better than the five above, they're worth a look — this list isn't the whole TECNO 5G catalog, just the five we think earn their price for most Nigerian buyers.  
  
## Buying cautions for Nigeria  
  
- **Confirm the RAM/ROM variant before paying.** TECNO sells multiple storage configurations under the same model name, and price differences between the 8GB and 12GB versions can exceed ₦40,000.  
- **Check the warranty channel.** Phones bought through Slot, Pointek, or Jumia's official TECNO store typically carry a valid Nigerian warranty; parallel imports from Jiji or open markets often don't.  
- **5G coverage is still city-first.** MTN and Airtel 5G is concentrated in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, and other major cities — outside those areas, a 5G phone will mostly run on 4G anyway, which is worth weighing against the Spark 30C 5G's lower price if you're not in a covered zone.  
- **Foldables need extra care.** If you're considering the Phantom V Flip2, factor in that foldable-specific screen repairs cost significantly more than a standard phone screen in Nigeria.  
  
## FAQ  
  
**Is TECNO 5G worth it if I mostly use WiFi at home?**  
Not urgently. If your data usage is mostly WiFi, the 5G radio adds little day-to-day value — you'd get more real-world benefit from prioritizing RAM, battery, and camera quality, which is where the Spark 30C 5G and Pova 7 5G already do well.  
  
**Which TECNO 5G phone has the best battery life?**  
The Pova 7 5G and Pova 7 Pro 5G both use a 6,000mAh cell, the largest on this list, making them the strongest picks for heavy daily use or travel days without a charger nearby.  
  
**Does the Camon 40 Pro 5G support MTN and Airtel 5G bands?**  
The Camon 40 Pro 5G supports 5G, but band compatibility depends on the specific model variant and can vary by region, so a chipset alone doesn't guarantee it'll connect to every operator's deployed bands. Confirm the exact Nigerian retail variant's supported bands — or ask the retailer directly — before buying if 5G connectivity is your main reason for the upgrade.  
  
**Is the Phantom V Flip2 5G officially sold in Nigeria?**  
TECNO's Phantom foldables have reached Nigerian retail before, but Flip2 stock is thinner and less consistent than the mainstream Spark, Pova, and Camon lines. Check current listings from an authorized TECNO retailer or store before assuming it's on the shelf — pricing also moves more with import costs, so expect to shop around more than you would for the other four phones here.  
  
**Should I buy a Pova 7 5G or wait for the Pova 8 series?**  
If you need a phone now, the Pova 7 5G is a safe buy at its current price. If you can wait a few months, newer Pova generations typically launch with better chipsets at similar starting prices, so patience usually pays off in this specific series.
