## Overview

itel has spent most of the last decade being the phone brand Nigerians buy when they want something that "just works" and don't want to think too hard about it. That reputation hasn't gone away in 2026, but the lineup underneath it has quietly gotten more interesting. There's now a genuine curved-AMOLED flagship wearing an itel badge, a phone that ships with its own charging case, and a mid-ranger built specifically around Unisoc's gaming-adjacent silicon — none of which would have sounded plausible from this brand five years ago.

The catch, as always with itel, is that naming is a maze. Super, S, P, A, Power, Zeno, Vision — the series names don't map cleanly to price or performance the way Samsung's A-to-S ladder does, and it's easy to end up comparing a 2023 model against a 2025 one without realizing it. So this guide sticks to what's actually current and sold in Nigeria right now, cross-checked against GSMArena listings and itel's own launch pricing, rather than repeating whatever a random retail listing happens to call "new."

## How we chose

The itel phones on this list were picked by weighing five things together: on-paper specifications (display, chipset, battery, cameras), confirmed software support where itel has actually published it, current Nigerian retail pricing across Jumia, Slot, and itel's own home stores, real availability rather than pre-order hype, and cross-referencing against GSMArena listings and hands-on coverage from outlets like GadgetMatch and GizGuide. A phone earns a spot here because it's genuinely worth the naira it costs relative to the alternatives on this list — not because it's the newest name in itel's catalog.

### Our overall pick

**itel Super 26 Ultra.** It's the first itel phone that doesn't feel like it's apologizing for its price tag — curved AMOLED, a proper 144Hz panel, IP65 resistance, and a 6,000mAh battery that doesn't force a trade-off against the screen quality. If your budget can't stretch that far, the S25 Ultra gets you most of the same screen experience for noticeably less.

This is itel's clearest signal yet that it wants to be taken seriously above the ₦150k mark. The Super 26 Ultra pairs a 6.8-inch curved AMOLED panel with a 144Hz refresh rate and a claimed 4,500 nits peak brightness, wrapped in Corning Gorilla Glass 7i and IP65 water and dust resistance — protection you'd normally expect a step or two up the price ladder. Under the glass sits a Unisoc T7300 chipset with 8GB of RAM, paired with a 6,000mAh battery on 18W charging, which is respectable rather than fast, but the capacity does most of the heavy lifting anyway.

The camera setup is modest for a phone wearing "Ultra" in its name — a 50MP main sensor with a 2MP depth helper on the back, and a 32MP selfie camera up front — so this isn't the pick if photography is your main criterion. What it's actually selling is the screen and the everyday feel of using it, and on that front it delivers more than its price suggests. It went on open sale in Nigeria from mid-September 2025, and pricing has held reasonably steady since.

**Skip this if:** you specifically want a strong camera system. The sensor hardware here is standard-fare budget itel, not a genuine step up from cheaper models in the lineup.

## itel S25 Ultra — the slim, cheaper alternative

The S25 Ultra was itel's previous AMOLED flagship, and it's aged into a genuinely good value pick now that the Super 26 Ultra has taken the top spot. It keeps a 6.78-inch AMOLED display at 120Hz, a Unisoc T620 chipset, 8GB of RAM, and a 5,000mAh battery — all wrapped into a body that's just 6.9mm thick, which is unusually slim for a budget-tier phone. It also carries an IP64 rating and an under-display fingerprint sensor, both of which were talking points when it launched and still hold up fine today.

If curved glass and the extra 24Hz of the Super 26 Ultra don't matter much to you, the S25 Ultra covers the same core experience — flagship-look AMOLED screen, decent battery, itel OS on Android 14 — for a meaningfully lower price, which makes it worth cross-shopping against what we covered in our [best Poco phone](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-poco-phone-2026/) guide at a similar budget tier.

**Skip this if:** you want the longest software runway. GSMArena's listing caps the S25 Ultra at two major Android upgrades, and itel hasn't published a clearer upgrade commitment for the Super 26 Ultra either — so neither phone is a long-term software bet, and you should go in expecting two OS updates at most from either one.

## itel Power 70 — the battery-anxiety cure

![itel Power 70 with detachable charging case](/assets/posts/itel-power-70.jpg)

The Power 70 exists to answer one question: what happens when NEPA takes light for two days straight? Its 6,000mAh internal battery already outlasts most phones in this price range, but the party trick is the bundled 4,000mAh charging case that snaps onto the back and pushes total capacity to a claimed 10,000mAh — itel markets it as Nigeria's first phone to hit that number. It runs on a MediaTek Helio G50 Ultimate chipset with up to 8GB of RAM and 256GB of storage, plus IP54 resistance and a four-year battery health guarantee, which is a rare commitment at this price point.

The trade-offs are what you'd expect: a basic 13MP main camera, an HD+ (not FHD+) display, and 18W charging that takes a while to top up a battery this size. But for students, traders, or anyone in an area with unreliable electricity, the calculation is simple — it launched in Nigeria in March 2025 at ₦124,900, and current retail listings put it anywhere from ₦110,000 to ₦170,000 depending on the RAM and storage variant.

**Skip this if:** camera quality or screen sharpness matters to your daily use. Both take a back seat to battery capacity here, same trade-off we flagged in our [best Tecno phone](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-tecno-phone-2026/) roundup for the Pova series.

## itel P65 — the gaming-focused mid-ranger

itel's P Series has always targeted light mobile gaming, and the P65 leans into that harder than any P-series phone before it, with an "iBoost" game engine and an optional mecha-styled charging case that adds 2,400mAh on top of the phone's own 5,000mAh battery. It runs a Unisoc T615 chipset — a meaningful step up over the older T606 used in the P55 — paired with 4GB to 8GB of RAM (extendable via memory fusion), a 6.7-inch 120Hz IPS LCD, and 18W fast charging with bypass-charging support, which is a genuinely useful feature for anyone who games while plugged in.

It's not a gaming flagship by any real measure, but for casual sessions of titles like the ones we cover in our [PUBG Mobile guides](https://www.revibyte.blog/pubgmobile/), it's a sensible step above the strictly-basics phones in itel's lineup, without pushing into Poco or Redmi territory on price.

**Skip this if:** you're chasing frame rates in demanding titles. The T615 handles casual and mid-tier games fine but isn't built for anything graphically heavy.

The A200 Plus is itel's newest entry-level play, launched in February 2026, and it's a meaningful step up from the A-series phones that came before it. It runs a Unisoc T7250 chipset — a real jump from the older T603 found in previous budget itel phones — paired with a 6.75-inch HD+ display at 120Hz, a 6,000mAh battery on 18W fast charging, and Android 15 (Go Edition) with itel's new "Sola AI" assistant baked in. It also carries IP65 dust and water resistance plus MIL-STD-810H military-grade durability certification, which is genuinely unusual at this price tier and puts it ahead of most budget rivals on build quality alone.

The trade-offs are the expected ones for the price: a basic 13MP main camera, no NFC, and no 5G — none of which matter much for a first smartphone or a backup device. Storage runs 64GB to 128GB with 3GB or 4GB of RAM, expandable via microSD. It's sold through Jumia and other major Nigerian retailers, and it's quickly becoming the A-series phone worth pointing budget buyers toward instead of older models still floating around in stores.

**Skip this if:** you want NFC for tap-to-pay, or a camera that does more than basic snapshots. The A200 Plus is built around battery life and durability, not photography.

## How to actually choose

- **Want the best screen itel makes:** Super 26 Ultra, or the S25 Ultra if the price gap matters more than 144Hz
- **Power cuts are a real problem where you live:** Power 70
- **You game casually and want headroom over the basics:** P65
- **Tight budget, first smartphone, or a backup device:** A200 Plus

If you're weighing itel against other budget-friendly brands before deciding, it's worth glancing at how [Honor](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-honor-phone-2026/) and [vivo](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-vivo-phones-nigeria-2026/) are pricing their entry tiers in Nigeria right now — the gap between them and itel has narrowed more than most buyers realize.

## FAQ

**Is the itel Super 26 Ultra worth it over the S25 Ultra?** If curved glass, the extra brightness, and a slightly faster refresh rate matter to you, yes. Otherwise the S25 Ultra delivers most of the same day-to-day experience for less money, and the core hardware gap between them is smaller than the price difference suggests.

**Does the itel Power 70's charging case really add 10,000mAh total?** itel's own figures put the internal battery at 6,000mAh and the case at 4,000mAh, adding up to 10,000mAh combined — but as with any manufacturer claim, treat the marketed "up to" figures on screen-on time as a best case rather than a guarantee.

**Are itel phones good for gaming in 2026?** For casual and mid-tier titles, the P65 is the most sensible pick in the lineup thanks to its T615 chipset and iBoost engine. None of itel's current phones are built for graphically demanding games — for that, you'd want to look outside the brand entirely.

**Where can I buy a genuine itel phone in Nigeria?** itel's official home stores, Jumia, Slot, and verified dealers around Computer Village are the safest bets. Always confirm the IMEI and box seal, especially on bundled models like the Power 70 that ship with an extra charging case.

**Which itel phone should a first-time smartphone buyer get?** The A200 Plus. It's newer than most of the budget phones it competes against, it carries genuine durability certification, and its 6,000mAh battery means it won't feel underpowered even for heavy daily use.
