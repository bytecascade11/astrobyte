## Overview 

Wireless charging on a Motorola phone is one of those features that sounds standard until you try to buy one. Motorola puts it on its premium Razr and Edge lines, sprinkles it across a few Moto G models, and then quietly removes it from a successor without announcing much. So the answer to "does my Motorola have it?" depends heavily on the exact model and year.

This guide is research-based, not hands-on. Specs come from Motorola's own product pages where possible, with GSMArena used as a secondary reference for older models.

It focuses mainly on Motorola's US-market models. Model names, availability and charging setups can differ in other regions, so check the exact model number before buying an imported phone in Nigeria.

## The short version

If you want wireless charging on a Motorola, look at the Razr family, the Edge (2024 and 2025), the Edge 50 Pro and Ultra, and the Moto G Stylus models from 2024 and 2025. The 2026 Moto G Power is the surprise omission: it charges by cable only, even though the 2025 model had the feature.

## Razr: foldables lead the way

Foldables get the best wireless charging on Motorola's list. The Razr Ultra (2026) and Razr Ultra (2025) both pair 68W wired charging with up to 30W wireless, which is a serious number for a flip phone. You need a 30W Motorola wireless charger to get it, though, as Motorola's own footnote on the Ultra page makes clear.

The Razr+ (2026) and standard Razr (2026) also support wireless charging, but Motorola's pages only mention a 15W charger for them and don't publish a maximum, so I'd avoid quoting a speed for either. Android Central reports that none of the three 2026 Razr models has built-in Qi2 magnets. You can still use a magnetic case and MagSafe-style accessories, but the alignment comes from the case rather than the phone.

If you're weighing the foldable route against a regular slab phone, our [Razr vs Edge vs Moto G comparison](https://www.revibyte.blog/motorola/motorola-razr-vs-edge-vs-moto-g/) covers the trade-offs beyond charging.

## Edge: the sensible middle

Both the Edge (2024) and Edge (2025) pair 68W wired charging with 15W wireless. Fifteen watts won't win any races, but it's what most people want from a bedside or desk pad: set the phone down, forget about it.

The Edge 50 Pro is the real outlier. GSMArena lists 125W wired, up to 50W wireless and 10W reverse wireless charging, all on a fairly small 4,500mAh battery. That wireless speed is more than three times what the 2024 and 2025 Edge phones manage. GSMArena lists the Edge 50 Ultra with the same 125W wired and 50W wireless figures, plus 10W reverse. Both phones were sold in many markets outside the US, which makes them more likely to turn up on Nigerian import listings than the US-only Edge models.

As with the Razr Ultra, the headline wireless speed only applies with a matching Motorola charger. A generic 15W pad will cap these phones well below 50W.

## Moto G: mostly no, with exceptions

This is where buyers get caught out. Most Moto G phones skip wireless charging to keep costs down, and the few that include it are easy to miss.

The Moto G Stylus (2025) is the clearest case: Motorola's own page lists a 5,000mAh battery, 68W wired charging and 15W wireless charging. The Moto G Stylus 5G (2024) and the Moto G Power (2025) also offer 15W wireless charging according to GSMArena, which means wireless charging isn't limited to Motorola's premium phones.

The bigger story is the Moto G Power (2026). [Android Central's review](https://www.androidcentral.com/phones/motorola/moto-g-power-2026-review) calls the missing wireless charging an odd omission given the 2025 model had it, and [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/2234300/best-motorola-phones-you-can-buy-in-2026-android/) reports the same. Best Buy's listing shows only 30W wired charging with a 5,200mAh battery. If you're upgrading from a 2025 Moto G Power and use a charging pad every night, that's worth knowing before you spend money.

Moto G Play (2026) and the standard Moto G (2026) are in the same camp: wired only as far as I can tell.

## What about Nigeria?

Many of these models aren't sold through official channels in Nigeria, so you may be importing or buying from third-party sellers. Wireless charging adds another variable there: regional variants can differ in charging support, and a charger bought locally may not match the phone's rated wattage. If you're importing, check the seller's reputation, the warranty and the exact model number before you pay.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does every Motorola phone support wireless charging?
No. It's mostly limited to the Razr, Razr+, Razr Ultra, Edge, and a small number of Moto G models. Most budget Moto G phones don't have it.

### Do the 2026 Razr phones support Qi2?
According to [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/phones/motorola/motorola-razr-2026-qi2-wireless-charging-support), no. The 2026 Razr, Razr+ and Razr Ultra lack built-in magnets, so there's no native Qi2 alignment, though magnetic cases work.

### Did the Moto G Power (2026) lose wireless charging?
Based on SlashGear's reporting and Best Buy's spec listing, yes. The 2026 model is wired-only, while the 2025 version had wireless.

### What wireless charger should I use?
A Qi-certified pad is the safe starting point. Check the wattage against your phone's rated wireless speed; a 15W pad will cap the faster Razr Ultra (2025) well below its 30W maximum.

### Can I use reverse wireless charging?
The Razr Ultra (2025) lists 5W reverse charging, useful for earbuds or a watch in a pinch. Other models vary, so confirm before relying on it.

### Is wireless charging worse for battery health?
It often runs warmer than wired charging, and heat isn't friendly to batteries over time. How warm depends on the charger's efficiency, how well the phone is aligned on the pad, and the conditions around it (a thick case or a hot room makes it worse). Manufacturers don't publish a simple "wireless costs you X%" figure, so I'd treat it as a convenience, not a lifespan booster.
