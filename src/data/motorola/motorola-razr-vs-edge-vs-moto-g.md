## Overview 

Motorola's catalog used to be confusing enough with two or three phones a year. Now there are three separate series running at once, and each one is chasing a different kind of buyer. If you've been staring at a Razr, an Edge, and a Moto G in the same browser tab wondering why the price gap is so wide, this is the breakdown that actually explains it.

## The Three Series, In Plain Terms

**Razr** is Motorola's foldable line. It's the one built around the flip-phone form factor, a cover display you actually use, and a price tag that reflects the hinge engineering inside it.

![Motorola Razr foldable phone open and closed, showing the cover display](/assets/motorola/motorola-razr-2026-lineup.jpg)

**Edge** is the standard slab flagship-adjacent line — big battery, sharp camera, premium build, but priced to undercut Samsung and Google rather than match them dollar for dollar.

![Motorola Edge 70 series phone showing thin profile and rear camera module](/assets/motorola/motorola-edge-70-series.jpg)

**Moto G** is the volume series. It's the tier where Motorola competes most directly with Infinix, Tecno, and itel on shelf price rather than specs sheets, and it tends to be the most widely stocked Motorola line in markets like Nigeria where price sensitivity drives most buying decisions.

## Who Should Actually Buy Which One

If your phone budget is driven by looking different from everyone else in the room, the Razr earns its price. The cover screen genuinely gets used for notifications, camera framing, and quick replies, and folding it shut still feels satisfying a year in. The trade-offs are a crease you'll notice and a battery that has less physical room to work with than a slab phone of the same price, since the fold and hinge take up space a conventional phone would give to cell capacity. Camera hardware on the Razr line has gotten genuinely capable, but it's still shaped by what fits around a folding chassis, so it doesn't always match what a similarly priced conventional flagship can pack in.

If you want the closest thing to a "normal" flagship experience without flagship pricing, the Edge series is the sensible middle path. It's the one we'd point most readers toward if camera consistency and update timelines matter as much as price — and we've covered this in more depth in our roundup of the [best Motorola camera phones of 2026](https://www.revibyte.blog/motorola/best-motorola-camera-phones-2026/).

If you're buying on a strict budget or replacing a phone that's on its last legs, Moto G is where Motorola's African and Nigerian market strategy really shows. It's also the series most likely to show up in our [best Motorola phones for battery life](https://www.revibyte.blog/motorola/best-motorola-phones-for-battery-life-2026/) picks, since Motorola has consistently fitted large-capacity batteries into this tier as a headline selling point.

One thing that applies across all three series: software support is set per model, not per series name, and it's worth confirming before you buy rather than assuming. A newer Razr or Edge flagship will typically carry a longer update commitment than an entry Moto G, but the exact number of OS versions and security-patch years still varies by specific model and by region — we broke this down fully in [how long Motorola phones get Android updates](https://www.revibyte.blog/motorola/how-long-do-motorola-phones-get-android-updates/).

If Motorola isn't feeling like the right fit after all this, it's worth cross-shopping against what else is out there at a similar price. Our [best OnePlus phones of 2026](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-oneplus-phone-2026/) guide covers the closest performance-per-dollar rival to the Edge series, while the [best Infinix phones of 2026](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-infinix-phone-2026/) list is the direct comparison point for anyone deciding between a Moto G and Infinix's Note or Zero lines. ## FAQ

**Is the Motorola Razr worth it over a regular Edge phone?**
Only if the folding form factor itself is the appeal. Spec for spec, the Edge series usually delivers more performance and camera consistency per dollar, since none of that budget goes toward hinge engineering.

**Which Moto G model is closest to an Edge phone in quality?**
The higher-numbered or "Power" and "Pro" variants in the Moto G line close the gap the most, particularly on display quality and battery capacity, though chipset performance still lags behind Edge-series phones.

**Do all three series run the same version of Android?**
They usually launch on the same or a similar Android version, but how many major OS versions and years of security patches a specific model actually receives is set individually and can vary even within the same series, so it's worth checking the exact model rather than assuming by series name.

**Which Motorola series is most commonly available in Nigeria?**
Moto G tends to be the easiest to find on Nigerian retail shelves, since it's the tier where Motorola prices most competitively against Infinix, Tecno, and itel. Edge and Razr models are available too, but typically through fewer retailers and often at a markup versus their US or European list price.

**Can I use a Razr as my only phone without any downsides?**
Mostly yes, though the crease, the camera compromises that can come with a folding design, and the smaller battery footprint are the main trade-offs.
