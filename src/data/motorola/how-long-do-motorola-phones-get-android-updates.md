## Overview 

If you've ever tried to pin down exactly how long a Motorola phone will keep getting Android updates, you already know the frustration. There's no single, clean answer — because Motorola's support commitment varies by model. Ask about a Moto G and you'll get a different answer than asking about a Razr, and even within the Razr family, the numbers can swing wildly depending on the exact model and the year it launched.

Here's the short version: **Motorola's Android support varies significantly by model.** Recent phones can receive anywhere from zero to three major OS upgrades, while security-patch windows range from roughly two years on entry-level devices to several years on select mid-range and flagship phones — with the Razr Fold and Motorola Signature advertised at seven years apiece. Where your specific phone lands depends on its price tier, launch year, and sometimes even your region, so treat lineup-level averages as a starting point, not a guarantee.

## Why Motorola's policy feels so inconsistent

Unlike Google or Samsung, which publish a single blanket commitment across most of their lineup, Motorola sets support windows on a phone-by-phone basis. A $250 Moto G and a $1,800 Razr Ultra are not treated the same, and even two similarly priced phones released a year apart can carry different promises. To make matters more confusing, Motorola's own documentation isn't always consistent across sources — press materials and regional support databases have, at times, listed different update counts or end dates for the same device. That's not just a communication problem for buyers; it's a real reason to double-check the specific support page for your model and region rather than relying on general marketing claims.

The one bright spot: Motorola has recently shown it *can* do long-term support well when it wants to. The company markets its Signature smartphone and the Razr Fold as receiving seven years of Android OS updates and seven years of security patches, putting them on par with Samsung's and Google's best flagship commitments — though as with other Motorola devices, it's worth confirming the figure against the official support page for your exact model, since regional listings for these same phones have occasionally shown shorter windows. The catch is that this level of support remains limited to a small part of Motorola's catalog.

## How to check your specific Motorola phone (do this before you buy or worry)

![Person checking Android version and update settings on a smartphone](/assets/motorola/checking-android-version-settings.jpg)

Given how much support windows differ from model to model — and even between different official sources for the same model — don't rely on general lineup averages, including the tables above, as your final word. Motorola itself directs users to its country-specific software-update lookup tool to confirm whether a given phone is still eligible for OS and security updates, and that's genuinely the most reliable source available:

1. Open **Settings > About phone > Android version** to confirm exactly which OS version and security patch level you're currently running.
2. Go to Motorola's official, country-specific support/software-update website and look up your **exact model name** (not just "Edge" or "Moto G" — the specific number and suffix matter).
3. Check the listed **end-of-life date** for security patches, not just the number of OS upgrades — a phone can be "out of upgrades" but still receiving security patches for another year or two, which matters more for day-to-day safety.
4. If two sources disagree (a retailer listing, a press release, and the support database all show different numbers), treat the official support database as the tiebreaker, and note the date you checked — these figures can be revised.
5. If you're buying new, subtract today's date from the promised end date to see how many years of real-world use you're actually getting, not just the headline number advertised at launch.

## FAQ

**Do all Motorola phones get the same number of updates?**
No. Support varies significantly by lineup and even by individual model year. The Razr Fold and Signature phone are advertised at up to seven years of updates, while many Moto G models get zero to one major OS upgrade.

**How many years of security updates does a typical Moto G get?**
Many recent Moto G phones receive around three years of security patches, but the exact window depends on the model, launch date, market, and sometimes carrier.

**Is the Motorola Razr's update support good?**
The standard Razr, Razr Plus, and Razr Ultra models have typically offered three major OS upgrades and roughly four years of security patches since 2023, though exact end dates vary by model and region. Either way, it's shorter than what most premium foldable rivals now offer.

**Why does the Razr Fold get more updates than other Razr models?**
Motorola markets the Razr Fold and Signature phone as top-tier flagship showcases with Samsung/Google-level support (seven years), while the mainstream Razr line has stayed at three OS upgrades since its debut. Worth noting: some regional support-database listings for the Razr Fold have shown shorter windows than the marketed seven years, so it's worth confirming the current figure for your specific region before assuming the longer number applies to your unit.

**Can I extend support after Motorola stops updating my phone?**
Some enthusiasts install unofficial custom ROMs like LineageOS to keep receiving security patches, but this requires technical know-how, isn't available for every model, and can void your warranty.

**Where can I find the official update timeline for my exact model?**
Check Motorola's official support and software update pages, which list device-specific end-of-life dates. Regional listings can differ, so confirm against the version sold in your country.
