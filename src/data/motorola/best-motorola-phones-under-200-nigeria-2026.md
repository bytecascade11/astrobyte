## Overview

Motorola doesn't get talked about nearly as much as Tecno or Infinix in the Nigerian budget phone conversation, and that's honestly a shame. The brand has quietly been shipping some of the cleanest, most bloat-free Android experiences you can get for under $200, and in a market where many budget phones come with extra apps and services you may never use, that's worth something.  
  
I checked current Nigerian listings across marketplaces such as Jiji and Jumia, while also accounting for the imported and UK-used stock commonly found in Nigeria, to put together this list. Using a rough August 2026 exchange-rate range, $200 is around ₦270,000–₦280,000, so everything here sits comfortably under or close to that ceiling, with a couple of picks well below it for anyone stretching a tighter budget.  
  
### How I picked these Motorola phones  
  
I prioritized Motorola models that can realistically be found through Nigerian importers and marketplaces under roughly $200, while weighing price, chipset performance, display quality, battery life, 5G support, storage, and overall value. Because Motorola's Nigerian availability is more limited than Tecno, Infinix, or Samsung, pricing and stock can change quickly, so treat the figures here as a snapshot rather than a fixed catalog.  
  
One thing worth flagging upfront: Motorola has a much smaller official consumer smartphone presence in Nigeria than brands such as Samsung, Tecno, and Infinix, so many Motorola phones on the local market come through importers and independent sellers rather than an official Motorola storefront. Prices swing depending on the seller, the phone's condition, and whether it's factory-unlocked. Treat the figures below as a realistic range, not a fixed price tag, and always confirm IMEI status and warranty terms before paying.

The G34 is the standout on this list because it combines 5G with a Snapdragon 695 chipset at a relatively low price. Snapdragon 695 is dependable, well-optimized, and handles multitasking better than the Helio G85 phones below it. The trade-off is the display: at 6.5 inches, a 720x1600 HD+ panel isn't particularly sharp by 2026 standards, and it's the weakest spec on an otherwise strong phone. If you're in an area with early MTN or Airtel 5G rollout and want future-proofing without paying Samsung 5G money — [our Samsung 5G under ₦300k guide](https://www.revibyte.blog/samsung/best-samsung-5g-phones-under-300/) covers that tier — the G34 is the smarter buy right now.  
  
## 2. Moto G Play (2026) — Newest Option, Best Battery and Display  
  
![Moto G Play 2026 front display showing 120Hz panel](/assets/motorola/moto-g-play-2026-nigeria.jpg)  
  
This is Motorola's freshest release in the lineup, and it shows. The MediaTek Dimensity 6300 chip brings 5G, a 5,200mAh battery (the biggest here), and a 120Hz 6.7-inch display. It has 4GB of physical RAM, with RAM Boost using storage as virtual memory to reach up to 12GB of combined available memory — worth knowing so you don't mistake that ceiling for actual RAM. It's currently the newest and most capable phone on this list on paper, though as a recent launch it's still working its way into Nigerian import channels, so pricing runs a bit higher than the G34 for now. If you can find a good deal on one, it's worth prioritizing over the older G Play models still floating around the secondhand market.  
  
## 3. Moto G24 — Best Cheap All-Rounder  
  
![Moto G24 90Hz display and 50MP camera](/assets/motorola/moto-g24-nigeria.jpg)  
  
This is the one I'd point most first-time Motorola buyers toward if 5G isn't a priority. It runs on the Helio G85, giving it a noticeable performance step up from the G04, while also offering 128GB of storage, a 90Hz display that makes scrolling noticeably smoother, and a 50MP main camera that handles daylight shots better than its specs sheet suggests. It's not a camera phone in the way ReviByte's [Tecno Camon picks](https://www.revibyte.blog/tecno/best-tecno-camera-phones-nigeria-2026/) are, but for casual Instagram posting it holds its own.  
  
## 4. Moto G04 — Cheapest Sensible Everyday Option  
  
![Moto G04 side profile showing fingerprint sensor](/assets/motorola/moto-g04-nigeria.jpg)  
  
The G04 sits a notch above the E14 with a Unisoc T606 chipset — the same efficient, budget-friendly chip found across several of Motorola's entry-level phones. What sets the G04 apart isn't raw power, it's Motorola's near-stock Android skin, which means fewer forced ads in the notification shade and faster updates than most rivals in this price range. A solid pick for students who mostly need WhatsApp, camera, and social media without lag.

If your ceiling is closer to ₦50,000 than ₦280,000, the Moto E14 is where Motorola's Nigerian lineup starts. The base variant ships with just 2GB of physical RAM — RAM Boost can use part of the phone's storage as virtual memory, though a 4GB physical RAM variant also exists in some markets, so confirm which one you're buying before paying. ## FAQ  
  
**Is Motorola a good brand to buy in Nigeria?**  
Motorola phones are reliable and generally well-built, but the smaller official consumer presence in Nigeria means after-sales support is thinner than what Tecno, Infinix, or Samsung offer. Buy from a seller who provides a warranty, and you should be fine.  
  
**Are these phones brand new or UK-used?**  
Both exist on the market. UK-used units are common and often cheaper, and quality UK-used stock is generally reliable if the seller is reputable — but always ask directly and check the battery health where possible.  
  
**Which of these 5 phones has the best camera?**  
The Moto G24 edges it out for daylight photography among the non-5G models thanks to its 50MP main sensor, though the newer Moto G Play (2026) also brings a capable 32MP main camera. None of these phones are strong low-light performers at this price point.  
  
**Can I get 5G under $200 from Motorola in Nigeria?**  
Yes — both the Moto G34 and the newer Moto G Play (2026) support 5G, making Motorola one of the few brands offering 5G phones anywhere near this price bracket in Nigeria.  
  
**How does Motorola compare to Tecno at this price?**  
Tecno generally wins on camera hardware and localized features for the Nigerian market — see our breakdown of the [best Tecno phones under $200](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-tecno-phones-under-200/) — while Motorola tends to win on software cleanliness and update consistency. It comes down to what you value more day-to-day.  
  
**Where should I buy a Motorola phone in Nigeria?**  
Jumia and Konga carry some models with buyer protection built in. If you're going through Jiji or a local market stall, insist on testing the IMEI, checking for iCloud/Google lock, and getting at least a short return window in writing.
