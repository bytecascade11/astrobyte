## Overview 

Motorola's cheapest phones used to be an easy recommendation: pick the newest Moto G, pay under $200, move on. That's gotten more complicated. The base Moto G and Moto G Power now both sit inside a $200-$300 range with listed prices that have moved more than once since launch, and spec sheets that overlap more than you'd expect for models $100 apart.

This isn't a "best Motorola phone" ranking — we already covered that ground in our [best Motorola phones of 2026 roundup](https://www.revibyte.blog/motorola/best-motorola-phones-2026-top-picks/). This is narrower: if your budget sits somewhere between $200 and $300, which specific model earns that money, and where does the extra spend actually buy something real.

## Quick answer

- **Around $200 — current generation:** Moto G (2026) — the straightforward, no-surprises current-generation option. Officially launched at $199.99.
- **$200-$250 — clearance value, only if available:** Moto G Power (2025), but only as new, unlocked stock from a reputable retailer. It's a discontinued model, so this window won't stay open indefinitely — when it's available, though, it undercuts the current base Moto G on screen sharpness and adds wireless charging.
- **Around $300 — current generation, worth the extra spend:** Moto G Power (2026) — pay more here specifically for the FHD+ display, 8GB of RAM, durability certification, and OIS, not for longer software support (more on that below).

Details and sourcing for each of these follow below.

A note on pricing: these are the figures confirmed at each phone's official launch or on current manufacturer/carrier listings. Retail and carrier promotions regularly move any of these by $20-$50 in either direction, and older-generation stock (like the 2025 Moto G Power) becomes harder to find new over time — treat this table as a reference point, not a live price check.

One model deliberately left out of that table: the **Moto G Play (2026)**. It launched at $169.99, well under this bracket, and Motorola's own listings have since shown it as high as $249.99 — but that jump isn't consistent across retailers the way the base Moto G and Moto G Power's pricing is, and some outlets still list it closer to its original price. Given that inconsistency, it doesn't fit cleanly into a $200-$300 comparison one way or the other, so it's left out of the recommendation below rather than force-fit into a bracket its normal street price doesn't reliably occupy.

## Why the price gaps don't map cleanly to performance

Look at the chipset column and the same MediaTek Dimensity 6300 appears across all three models in this comparison, despite a $100 difference between the base Moto G and Moto G Power (2026). That's confirmed across Motorola's own product pages and independent spec databases for both Power generations. The processor isn't what separates these phones.

What actually changes between them is screen resolution (HD+ versus FHD+), RAM (4GB versus 8GB), battery capacity, and durability certification. None of that is trivial — 8GB of RAM and a sharper display are real, usable differences — but it's a narrower set of upgrades than the price jump might suggest.

## Cameras: a real difference, but a modest one

Both current Moto G models covered here use 50MP main cameras, but the camera hardware and stabilization differ significantly between them. Only the Moto G Power (2026) adds optical image stabilization to its main sensor — confirmed on Motorola's own product page and corroborated by GSMArena's spec listing. OIS genuinely helps in dim, handheld shots, which is where budget phones usually struggle most. The base Moto G uses a simpler setup without stabilization. (The Moto G Play, left out of this comparison for the pricing reasons above, uses a different single-camera setup entirely and isn't covered here.)

None of these compete with camera-first budget phones from other brands. If photography is the priority rather than a nice-to-have, our [Oppo camera guide](https://www.revibyte.blog/oppo/oppo-camera-guide-australia-choosing-the-right-phone-for-photos/) and [Xiaomi camera comparison](https://www.revibyte.blog/xiaomi/xiaomi-cameras-compared-redmi-poco-xiaomi/) both cover phones that push harder on imaging at comparable price points, though availability varies by region.

## Software support: the same for the base Moto G and Moto G Power

This is worth being precise about, because it's easy to assume the pricier phone gets better long-term support. It doesn't. According to Motorola's own product listings and confirmed independently by GSMArena, Tom's Guide, and 9to5Google in their respective reviews, the Moto G (2026) and Moto G Power (2026) carry the same commitment: two major Android OS upgrades and three years of total security updates from launch. (The Moto G Play shares this same policy, for what it's worth, even though its pricing puts it outside this particular comparison.)

That means the extra $100 you'd spend moving from the base Moto G to the Moto G Power buys better hardware, not a longer software runway. If update longevity matters more to you than screen resolution or camera stabilization, none of these three phones changes that calculation — you'd need to look outside Motorola's 2026 Moto G lineup entirely. Our full [Motorola Android update support guide](https://www.revibyte.blog/motorola/how-long-do-motorola-phones-get-android-updates/) covers how that compares across Motorola's broader lineup, including the Edge and Razr families, and why support windows vary phone-by-phone rather than following a blanket company policy.

One caveat: this two-OS-upgrade, three-year-security commitment applies to the 2026 models specifically. If you're considering the discounted 2025 Moto G Power instead, don't assume it carries the identical window — check that model's specific update commitment before buying, since Motorola sets these on a per-phone basis and older listings aren't always kept current.

## The Moto G Power (2025) trade — worth checking before buying new

One of the more counterintuitive options in this bracket is buying the previous generation instead of the current one. The 2025 Moto G Power shipped with a 6.8-inch FHD+ display, 8GB of RAM, and — notably — 15W wireless charging, a feature Motorola removed entirely from the 2026 model. Reviewers covering the 2025-to-2026 update have specifically flagged the loss of wireless charging as a downgrade rather than a lateral change.

If you can still find a new, unlocked Moto G Power (2025) from a reputable retailer at a discounted price — rather than a used or refurbished unit — it can be a genuinely better buy than the current base Moto G (2026), since it offers a sharper screen and wireless charging that the newer, similarly-priced base model doesn't have. New stock of a discontinued model does dry up over time, though, so this option won't stay available indefinitely.

## Moto G (2026) vs. Moto G Power (2026): the $100 question

| Spec | Moto G (2026) | Moto G Power (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Display | 6.7" HD+ LCD, 120Hz | 6.8" FHD+ LCD, 120Hz, up to 1000 nits |

The takeaway: since update support is identical between these two, the $100 gap comes down entirely to hardware — sharper screen, more RAM, real durability certification, and a stabilized camera. Both are 5G-capable — this is confirmed in the band listings on GSMArena and Motorola's own product pages for each model.

## FAQ

**Is the $300 Moto G Power actually better than the $200 base Moto G, or just marketing?**
Genuinely better on hardware: the sharper FHD+ display, 8GB of RAM, IP68/IP69 rating, and stabilized main camera are confirmed upgrades, not a repackaged spec sheet. What it doesn't include is longer software support — both phones carry the same two-OS-upgrade, three-year-security commitment.

**Should I buy a discounted 2025 Moto G Power instead of the new base Moto G?**
It's worth considering if you can find new, unlocked stock at a discount from a reputable retailer. The 2025 model has a sharper FHD+ screen and wireless charging that the 2026 base Moto G doesn't offer, though you should confirm that specific unit's remaining update window before buying, since it wasn't verified as part of this guide.

**Do these phones support 5G?**
Yes — the Moto G (2026) and Moto G Power (2026) both list 5G band support on their official spec sheets.

**Which model has the best camera in this price range?**
The Moto G Power (2026) is the strongest of the models covered here thanks to optical image stabilization on its main sensor, which the base Moto G doesn't include.

**Is it worth waiting for a sale instead of buying at listed price?**
Often yes. Prices on both current models have moved since launch, and carrier promotions or trade-in deals regularly bring them below MSRP — check current listings before assuming the launch price is what you'll actually pay.

**Do the Moto G and Moto G Power get different software update timelines?**
No — as of their 2026 launches, both carry the same commitment from Motorola: two major Android OS upgrades and three years of total security updates. Confirm this hasn't changed by checking the specific model's listing before buying, since Motorola's support windows are set per phone and can vary by region.
