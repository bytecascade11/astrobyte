## Overview 
  
Right back isn't a glamour position. Nobody's coming to your stream to watch a fullback make a recovery tackle. But ask anyone who's been turned inside-out by a flying winger in Division 1 — the RB slot can decide a match before halftime. In eFootball 2026, pace alone doesn't cut it at this position anymore. You need someone who can defend, push forward when the game allows, and still track back before your center backs start sending you messages.  
  
This list comes from actual online Division matches across multiple formations — not just stat sheets. Every player below was tested in real match conditions, and the differences between them become very obvious once you hit Divisions 3 and above.  
  
> **Card Version Notice:** All ratings and stats in this article refer to **standard base cards available in the June 2026 update**. Highlight, Epic, and Big Time versions of these players will have different — often significantly higher — values. If you're running a boosted card, treat the numbers below as a floor, not a ceiling.

## What Makes a Good RB in eFootball 2026?  
  
Before we get into names, let me be clear about what I'm evaluating. In eFootball 2026, the right back role splits into two rough profiles depending on your formation:  
  
**The Defensive-First RB** — You want this guy if your wingers love to bomb forward and leave you exposed. He stays wide, tracks overlapping runs, and wins his one-on-ones. He won't bag assists regularly but he'll save you from conceding the kind of goals that make you quit the match early.  
  
**The Wing-Back Hybrid** — Some RBs in this game genuinely function like a right midfielder when you're in possession. Great for asymmetric formations like 4-3-3 or 4-2-3-1 where the right side needs to carry width and depth at the same time.  
  
The stats that actually move matches at this position: **Defensive Awareness**, **Speed**, **Stamina**, **Ball Control**, and **Low Pass**. Don't let anyone convince you crossing matters more than defensive awareness in Division play — the evidence just doesn't support it.

## Player Breakdowns  
  
### 1. Trent Alexander-Arnold — The Brain of the Backline  
  
TAA on his Real Madrid standard card is genuinely one of the best RBs in the game if you're running a possession-heavy system. His passing from the right side is elite — crossing, low pass, through ball — and eFootball's AI actually uses his vision in a way that makes him feel different to control compared to every other fullback here. Defensively he won't bully pace merchants, so pair him with a covering CB on that side and set your right CM to "come back on defense."  
  
If you're running a [4-3-3 formation](/efootball/efootball-2026-4-3-3-formation-guide-division-1/) that leans on build-up play from the back, TAA is the pick nobody else on this list can replicate.  
  
**Best formation fit:** 4-3-3, 4-2-3-1    
**Best for:** Build-up play, possession teams, dictating tempo from the back  
  
---  
  
### 2. Achraf Hakimi — The Fastest Option, Full Stop  
  
Hakimi's standard PSG card has the engine no other RB touches right now. 96 speed means he recovers from positions he genuinely shouldn't survive — which matters because his defensive positioning AI isn't the sharpest. In attack he's a constant threat: overlaps without prompting, wins wide free kicks by simply getting there first, and his finishing from wide angles surprises opponents who stop tracking him after ten minutes.  
  
If you like running in behind and want your right side feeling threatening on every transition, Hakimi is the pick. Pair him with a [fast right winger](/efootball/best-wingers-efootball-2026/) who tracks back when you're defending — the combination on the right flank becomes a genuine problem for most opponents.  
  
**Best formation fit:** 3-4-3, 4-3-3 with a defensively disciplined right winger    
**Best for:** Counter-attack builds, high-tempo pressing, overlapping combinations  
  
---  
  
### 3. Reece James — The Complete Package  
  
James on his standard Chelsea card doesn't top any single stat but he's the most well-rounded RB on this entire list. His defensive awareness combined with genuine physicality means he wins tackles that pure pace players lose, and he tracks diagonal runs better than the game's AI typically handles. Going forward he's controlled and reliable rather than flashy — no surprises, no disasters, just a fullback who actually does what you need him to do.  
  
For players who want to slot in an RB and forget about it, James is the answer. He also fits naturally into the [best CB pairings](/efootball/best-cb-pairings-efootball-mobile-2026/) on the right side of a back four because his defensive positioning tightens the whole line.  
  
**Best formation fit:** 4-4-2, 4-2-3-1    
**Best for:** Set-and-forget reliability, consistent defensive output across all opponent types

### 4. Kyle Walker — Old Legs, Still Relevant  
  
Walker's standard Manchester City card still has 95 speed, which is genuinely absurd for a player his age, and the defensive shape that comes with those stats is tighter than most younger options. He handles physical duels well and rarely gets dragged out of position by pacey wingers because he's often faster than they are anyway. Offensively he's limited — don't design a system around his attacking output — but as a defensive right flank enforcer he earns every place on this list.  
  
**Best formation fit:** 4-4-2, 5-3-2    
**Best for:** Low-block setups, neutralizing quick left wingers, defensive-first teams  
  
---  
  
### 5. Pedro Porro — The Most Slept-On Card in This Tier  
  
Porro's standard Tottenham card might be the most undervalued RB in the June 2026 meta. His stamina is legitimately exceptional — he doesn't fade in the second half the way most attacking fullbacks do — and his AI movement finds pockets of space on the right without you manually directing him there. Against elite opponents he can struggle to contain truly rapid left wingers one-on-one, but for the Division 5 through Division 2 grind he consistently outperforms his overall rating.  
  
The GP cost makes him even more compelling. You're getting most of what James or Hakimi give you on the right side at a fraction of the price.  
  
**Best formation fit:** 4-3-3, 4-1-4-1    
**Best for:** High-tempo play, budget builds, teams needing the right side active for a full 90 minutes

### 6. Diogo Dalot — Honest, Affordable, Gets the Job Done  
  
Dalot's standard Manchester United card is the honest answer when someone asks: "Who's the best RB I can get without killing my GP budget?" Nothing spectacular, nothing that'll directly cost you a match. Decent pace, reasonable defensive reads, the occasional attacking contribution. He's not a Division 1 stopper, but in Divisions 4 through 6 he's dependable in a way that matters more than you'd expect.  
  
**Best formation fit:** 4-2-3-1, 4-4-2 flat    
**Best for:** New team builders, GP-limited rosters, casual multiplayer  
  
---  
  
### 7. Denzel Dumfries — Counter-Attack Machine  
  
Dumfries' standard Inter Milan card works best in teams built for quick vertical transitions. He overlaps aggressively the moment you win the ball back and his stamina means he's still doing it late in the second half. The weakness is consistent and visible: he reads crosses poorly coming into his box and can lose his man in set-piece situations where positioning matters more than recovery speed.  
  
Use him in systems that give him defensive cover — a holding midfielder who shifts right, or a back three where a CB can cover his channel. When he has that protection, the output going forward is worth it.  
  
**Best formation fit:** 3-5-2, 4-3-3 with a holding midfielder covering the right channel    
**Best for:** Counter-press teams, players who want offensive width with regular overlap contributions  
  
---  
  
### 8. Vanderson — The Wildcard Pick  
  
Vanderson's standard Monaco card has the second-fastest pace on this list at 91 and some genuinely surprising dribbling stats for a fullback. He's not for everyone: his defensive awareness at 77 will leave you badly exposed in Division 1 if you're not actively covering the space behind him. But in a high-press system where the whole squad defends forward together, he becomes a right flank problem that opponents genuinely struggle to read. Nobody expects a fullback to beat them off the dribble — and then he does.  
  
**Best formation fit:** 4-3-3 high-press, 3-4-3    
**Best for:** Experimental setups, wide dribbling combinations, high-risk high-reward play

## My Honest Take  
  
**Reece James is the most reliable RB for most players. Hakimi is the most fun if you want to attack down the right.** TAA is the most interesting option for possession-focused teams, but he genuinely costs you if you're not protecting the space behind him — don't use him without a right midfielder set to track back, and don't run him in a formation that leaves the right flank exposed.  
  
Porro and Vanderson are the two cards most players overlook right now, and both are at prices that make testing them easy. Porro's stamina changes how the right side plays in the final 20 minutes of tight matches — that's where he earns his spot. Vanderson's dribbling creates situations that purely defensive fullbacks never produce, and in a high-press system that counts for a lot.  
  
One thing worth saying clearly: don't over-invest in crossing stats for your RB. Defensive awareness and pace consistently matter more at every level below Division 1 Elite. If you're choosing between two cards with similar overalls, take the one with better defensive awareness every time.

## Frequently Asked Questions  
  
**Who is the best RB for possession play in eFootball 2026?**    
Trent Alexander-Arnold on his standard Real Madrid card. His low pass (92) and crossing (91) are midfield-level numbers on a fullback, and eFootball's AI uses his vision to find passes that no other RB on this list attempts. If your system is built around keeping the ball through the thirds, nobody else here does what he does from that position.  
  
**Who is the best pure defensive RB in eFootball 2026?**    
Reece James. His defensive awareness (87) combined with physical stats that win both aerial and ground duels gives him the most consistent defensive output across all match situations. Walker has more raw speed, but James reads the game better and positions himself before danger arrives rather than recovering after.  
  
**Who is the best attacking RB in eFootball 2026?**    
Achraf Hakimi, and it's not close. His 96 speed and overlapping AI make him a constant offensive presence — he contributes from wide in ways that pure fullbacks simply don't. The trade-off is the defensive space he leaves, which becomes very real when you're facing a fast left winger in the top divisions.  
  
**Who is the best RB under 50,000 GP?**    
Pedro Porro. His standard Tottenham card sits well below the cost of the top-tier options, and his stamina (90) plus reliable defensive stats make him a genuine performer across mid-to-high division play. He's the budget pick that doesn't feel like a compromise after the first few matches.  
  
**Does card version matter — standard vs Highlight vs Epic?**    
Yes, significantly. This entire article covers standard base cards only. A Highlight Hakimi or Epic Reece James will have noticeably higher stats and often different skill traits or playing style bonuses. If you're comparing a standard card from this list against a boosted version, the boosted card almost always wins on raw numbers alone.  
  
**Which card type should I aim for at RB?**    
Start with a standard Reece James or Porro, use them to learn the position and fine-tune your defensive instructions, then upgrade to a Highlight or Epic version when you're pushing into Division 2 or 1. Epic RBs are genuinely impactful offensively, but at most division levels a well-set-up standard card outperforms a flashy Epic card with the wrong instructions.
