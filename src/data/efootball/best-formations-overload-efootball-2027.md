## Overview 

Two different things get called "overload" in eFootball 2027, and mixing them up leads to bad setups. Konami confirmed **Overload** as a new selectable Team Playstyle in the v6.0.0 Season Update (released August 13, 2026) — it concentrates your players on the ball side to create numerical superiority in one area, both in and out of possession. Separately, "overload" is also just a general tactical idea: any time you're building extra bodies into one zone, formation-based or not, whether or not you've selected that specific playstyle. This guide covers both, and is clear about which is which for each setup.

## What actually creates an overload

Formation alone doesn't guarantee a numerical advantage — that depends on how your players are positioned when the ball arrives, which Playing Styles they're running (Konami confirmed v6.0.0 gives every player separate attacking and defensive Playing Styles, rather than one style covering both phases), and how the opponent's AI reacts. The underlying pattern the Overload playstyle is built around is a local triangle: a ball-side full-back, a nearby midfielder, and a wide player combining in tight space, while one player on the far side is meant to stay wide as an outlet for when the defense compresses. The numbers on paper (3v2, 2v1) describe the intent of the shape, not a guaranteed in-match outcome — individual instructions and opponent shape still decide whether it actually happens.

The intent: the full-back, CMF, and winger on the ball side form the local triangle shown above; the DMF is meant to sit central to protect the switch instead of joining the crowd; the far winger is meant to stay wide as the release valve. How closely that holds up in a given match depends on your players' individual instructions and how the opponent presses.

| Role | What to prioritize | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Ball-side full-back | Stamina, passing, defensive awareness | Supports the triangle, recovers after loss |
| Holding DMF | Positioning, interception | Protects the switch, recycles possession |
| Ball-side CMF | Tight possession, low pass, balance | Connects under pressure |
| Far winger | Speed, awareness, 1v1 ability | Stretches the block, receives the switch |
| CF | Link play or box movement | Pins center-backs, finishes combinations |

**How to use it:** Build possession down one side with the full-back, CMF, and winger using one-touch passes only when there's a forward-facing option. Once the opponent has committed four or more defenders to that side, don't force it — recycle through the DMF or switch immediately to the far winger, then attack the box while their back line is still shifting across.

**Weakness / counter:** The far side is exposed if both full-backs push up at once. Opponents beat this by switching play early rather than dribbling through the crowd, and by attacking the channel behind your advanced full-back once your press is broken.

The diagram above shows the mechanics: defend in a compact 4-2-3-1, then in possession one full-back (say the right-back) pushes forward to join the front line, while the opposite full-back drops in alongside the two center-backs to form a back three. The AMF and both wide players stay high, so the front line effectively becomes four (AMF, both wingers, advancing full-back) with one CF ahead of them, and the two holding midfielders stay put as the pivot. The intended attacking shape is roughly a 3-2-4-1; it reverts to the original 4-2-3-1 the instant you lose the ball.

**How to use it:** Keep the double pivot split between one ball-winner and one distributor — not two players who both vacate the middle. The AMF should prioritize awareness and passing over pace, since their job is connecting midfield to the CF rather than making runs themselves. Which full-back advances is up to you — pick whichever one has the better stamina and crossing.

**Weakness / counter:** If the switch back to defensive shape is slow, the lone CF gets isolated and the back three can be caught wide. This is the lowest-risk option here specifically because the defensive shape doesn't change.

## 3. 4-2-1-3 with the Overload Playstyle (Best Against Low Blocks)

The more aggressive alternative to setup #1. Three forwards give you immediate presence high up the pitch, and the AMF is positioned to attack second balls and loose clearances rather than just circulate possession.

**How to use it:** Best against opponents already sitting deep, since you have more bodies committed forward from kickoff. Keep the double pivot conservative and avoid sending both full-backs forward simultaneously — with three forwards already committed, there's less defensive cover if the ball is lost in transition.

**Weakness / counter:** The gap between the AMF and the double pivot is the target. Quick, direct opponents exploit this immediately after winning the ball back.

## 4. 4-3-3, Manual Full-Back Overlap (Possession-Based)

Same base shape as setup #1, but without relying on the Overload playstyle's automated ball-side concentration — instead, you manually commit one full-back forward on the flank where your winger and CMF are individually strongest, rather than letting the system decide. Useful if your squad is genuinely lopsided (a much stronger right winger than left, for example).

**How to use it:** Pick a side based on your actual player profiles, not habit. If your right winger has better close control and your right full-back has better stamina, build there every time and use the left side purely as an outlet, not a secondary attacking option.

**Weakness / counter:** Because it's a manual, human-decided overload rather than a system-supported one, discipline matters more — a distracted defensive full-back on the overloaded side is the single biggest way this breaks down.

## 5. 4-2-2-2, Manual Central Overload (Highest Risk)

Worth flagging clearly, and differently from the other four setups: this one isn't built around a confirmed game mechanic like the Overload playstyle or Fluid Formation. It's a manual, experimental approach — two center-forwards plus two narrow creative players trying to collect knockdowns and second balls centrally. Treat it as the least-proven option on this list rather than a systematic recommendation.

**How to use it:** Only worth trying against opponents who sit back and rarely counter fast, since your full-backs need to choose overlapping runs carefully rather than as a default. If you try it, it's worth judging for yourself over a handful of matches before relying on it — this is the setup here with the least established track record.

**Weakness / counter:** Any opponent with pace in wide areas will exploit the total lack of width. This is the setup to avoid in competitive online matches unless you're already controlling the game.

## A note on sourcing

The Overload Team Playstyle, Fluid Formation, and the split between attacking and defensive Playing Styles are all features Konami confirmed in the v6.0.0 patch notes. Setups #1 through #4 build directly on those confirmed mechanics. Setup #5 does not — it's a manual approach without an equivalent official mechanic behind it, and is labeled as such above. The role priorities, "how to use it" steps, and weaknesses for each setup are tactical judgment calls, not lines quoted from Konami — treat them as a starting point to test in Training or Friend Match rather than settled fact.

## FAQ

**What is Overload in eFootball 2027?**
It's a Team Playstyle introduced in v6.0.0 that concentrates players on the ball side to create numerical superiority in one zone, while trying to stay compact enough to press quickly if possession is lost.

**Do I need to select the Overload playstyle to build an overload?**
No. Setups #2, #4, and #5 above create similar numerical advantages through formation and Fluid Formation without using the Overload playstyle itself.

**Which setup is best for beginners?**
4-2-3-1 into 3-2-4-1 via Fluid Formation (setup #2). The defensive shape doesn't change, so it's the most forgiving if your timing on switches isn't sharp yet.

**Is the central overload (4-2-2-2) too risky for online matches?**
For most matchups, yes — it gives up touchline width completely. It's more reliable when you're already dictating the game against an opponent who won't counter quickly.

**Do these setups still work after the v6.0.0 update?**
Yes, these are v6.0.0-era setups, though how effective specific runs and presses feel can vary with ongoing balance changes and your opponent's own Playing Styles.
